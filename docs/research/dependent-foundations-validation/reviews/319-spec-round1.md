## Statement review and connected cases

I checked the assumptions, quantifiers, definitions and conclusions of every new formal claim in both Lean files. All generic base types and payload families use the declared universe `u`; this is a scope restriction, not concealed cross-universe generality. No `DecidablePred P` or `DecidablePred Q`, arbitrary comparison record, or extra axiom is assumed. Classical model-to-section selection comes from the existing `Model.toSection`; concrete selected sections and the explicit comparison do not decide an arbitrary predicate.

The claims fall into the following connected groups:

- Formation and reindexing (`Setting` through `map_comp`, library lines 18–49) use the inherited finite-presentation formation. `reindex` substitutes Q along `totalMap P`, so its dependency changes with the first context. The strict equalities follow from these split definitions. `expected_zero`, `expected_successor`, `no_first_left_zero` and `second_really_depends` establish nonconstant, inhabited cases and rule out a constant-fiber reading.
- The pullback structure, extensionality, comparison inverse laws, projections, identity/composition, rebase laws and `formed_isPullback` (lines 51–127) compare whole values over their actual base equations. Existence and uniqueness are constructed, rather than restating Boolean observations. The categorical pullback specialization at example lines 108–112 uses the general theorem. Equality certificates live in Prop, so proof irrelevance is appropriate to the declared extensional setting.
- The categorical setting operations (lines 129–165) instantiate Mathlib's object-and-morphism slice equivalence and pullback functor. `setting_term_twice` quantifies over every display map and section into the nested total. Its inherited `Family.termSub_composition` compares the iterated pullback section through the canonical composition isomorphism. Inspection of `FamilyCoherence` confirms separate naturality, unit and associativity results for that canonical pullback representation; arbitrary chosen pullbacks are not asserted definitionally equal.
- `Ty`, type/term substitution, their identity/composition laws, `type_comparison`, `term_comparison` and `dependent_comparison_comp` (lines 167–212) retain an arbitrary dependent result. The Sigma conclusion includes the total-space value and its value in the corresponding fiber. The `Fin` payload at example lines 29–37 varies with the second choice and base index. Lines 114–155 consume the public type/term and composite comparison results, including the whole pair with payload 6 in `Fin 7`. The numerical check is consistent with the frozen substitution order: shift after double gives original index 5 at input 2, then payload 6. At input 0, reversing the order gives payloads 2 and 3.
- `Argument`, `classify`, `normalize`, `recover`, their laws and `argumentEquiv` (lines 214–262) establish both directions of the universal section interpretation. Normalization transports the whole pair along the base equation. `liftClassifying` and `argumentOverEquiv` (lines 264–297) extend the bijection to every substitution `Y → X`, with both inverses proved. The example uses both the ordinary and substituted correspondence.
- `argumentOfModels`, both model-recovery results and both generic-recovery results (lines 299–321) connect selected sections to the existing finite geometric models. The second model's type depends on the graph of the selected first section. The example at lines 59–67 uses both recoveries. These results do not claim classification of arbitrary source topoi or arbitrary geometric morphisms.
- `argumentSub`, its unit/composition laws, `classify_sub` and `selected_comparison` (lines 323–345) preserve the selected dependent argument. `selected_twice` and `whole_selected` consume the public results. The separately proved original-index calculation checks the intended order rather than substituting a tag-only claim for it.
- The erasure and rejection claims (example lines 157–178) are nonvacuous: the selected value at 1 has both left tags, while erasure retains the base and chooses right. `no_second_left` quantifies over every second fiber value over first-right. `cannot_repair_second` therefore rejects the promised second observation, not merely this particular implementation of erasure.

The new `rfl` proofs are justified by the explicit split interpretation or by the underlying model construction. I found no circular hypothesis, impossible premise used as a positive example, dependency silently replaced by a constant family, or mismatch between the named observation and its actual conclusion. Native equality, categorical isomorphism/equivalence and interpreted finite-scheme satisfaction have distinct roles here. No new object-language higher identity or contextual J claim is made.

## Primary sources and interpretation limits

The candidate uses this as context for the display-map representation; it does not claim to instantiate the entire natural-model theorem for every existing project model.

I rechecked [Stacks Project Lemma 7.30.1](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/04GY): slicing a sheaf topos at a sheaf yields a topos, with the stated localization inverse image. The report's scope matches this result. The checked dependency is Mathlib's iterated-slice equivalence, whose forward/inverse functors and unit/counit I inspected locally at the pinned revision.

I rechecked [MTT §§2, 4–5, Figure 3 and Definition 5.1](https://www.danielgratzer.com/papers/multimodal-dependent-type-theory.pdf). Contravariant locks and the separately supplied mode/model structure support the report's distinction between modal access and internal setting formation. No modal admission theorem is borrowed to discharge the nested construction.

The positive decision is limited to the specified finite geometric scheme interpreted in Type/Set families. It includes arbitrary predicates and substitutions within that base, full selected dependent data, actual categorical slices, and universal section interpretation. It does not establish arbitrary internal sites, a general universe of theories, generated-source adequacy, cross-model higher transport or ergonomic superiority. Those limits are stated in the fixture/report and do not weaken #319's selected acceptance case.
