# Torsors and quotients on the doubled-origin chart site

The comparison in [Examples/SheafSiteComparison](../../Examples/SheafSiteComparison.lean)
changes the site of the existing quarter-turn clutching torsor. Its source is
the category of sections in either represented chart of the doubled-origin
sheaf. Its target is the element site of that sheaf. The source retains every
arrow between image sections, including arrows between different chart labels.
The induced topology makes this inclusion a fully faithful dense subsite.

This gives equivalences of the complete torsor and action-quotient categories,
including all arrows, for every parameter sheaf on the target site. The
equivalences preserve the group action, total display, projection and equivariant
target map. They commute with parameter restriction and effective covering
descent. The result concerns this explicit comparison and the stated dense
subsite hypotheses; it asserts no general Morita invariance or equivalence of
the underlying site categories.

## The sites and hypotheses

Let `J` be the smooth presentation topology and `X` the doubled-origin sheaf
`SmoothClutching.base`. Put `D = ElementSite.Site X.obj`, with its inherited
topology `K`. For the two chart maps `f i : Y i ⟶ X`,
[ChartElementSite](../../SquareKit/ChartElementSite.lean) defines `A` as the
induced category on `Σ i, ElementSite.Site (Y i).obj`. The functor `F : A ⥤ D`
sends each chart section to its image. Fullness and faithfulness follow from
the induced-category construction. Its topology is `F.inducedTopology K`.

`down_coverByImage` identifies the sieve generated by images of `F` with the
joint image sieve of the chart family. Consequently `cover_dense_iff` proves
that density is equivalent to joint local surjectivity. The existing actual
two-chart cover supplies that hypothesis. Mathlib's induced-topology comparison
then supplies continuity and cocontinuity.

The structured comparison uses `C D : Type u` with `Category.{u}` structures,
`Type u` sheaves and `GrpCat.{u}` group sheaves. Thus the selected small sites
and fibers occupy one universe; the sheaf and torsor categories themselves may
be larger. The small limits needed by Mathlib's `Type u` sheaf comparison are
available. No representability assumption is imposed on `X`, and no claim is
made that the doubled-origin sheaf is represented by a smooth presentation.

Forward restriction needs continuity and cocontinuity. Full categorical
recovery additionally needs fullness, faithfulness and `IsDenseSubsite`. These
are explicit assumptions of the generic equivalence and proved for the chart
consumer. The weaker forward assumptions also permit the empty-site failure
control to use exactly the same restriction construction.

## Structured comparison and recovery

[ElementSiteComparison](../../SquareKit/ElementSiteComparison.lean) proves that
the comparison remains fully faithful and dense over any presheaf of parameters.
The key new sieve calculation commutes projection with pushforward. This lets
each dependent torsor family use the dense-subsite sheaf equivalence on its
own parameter element site.

[SheafSiteComparison](../../SquareKit/SheafSiteComparison.lean) restricts the
group, target, action, parameter and dependent family componentwise. Total
spaces and projections restrict exactly. Joint local surjectivity is preserved
by cocontinuity. The
[torsor](../../SquareKit/SheafSiteComparisonTorsor.lean) and
[quotient](../../SquareKit/SheafSiteComparisonQuotient.lean) modules construct
the resulting functors and prove full faithfulness and essential surjectivity.
The resulting equivalences include multiplicative equivalences of automorphism
groups. Quotient arrow recovery checks the target equation by dense section
extensionality; it does not discard the target and recover only a torsor.

The inverse torsor construction reuses #665's `PrincipalSheaf.extend`,
`restrict_extend` and `equivariant_iff` from `PrincipalSheafLocalEquivalence`,
together with its `section_ext` for target equivariance. These transport the
action, principality and local inhabitation and reflect equivariance through
the lifted sheaf equivalence. Fullness of that equivalence lifts the actual
arrows and target maps. Mathlib supplies the underlying sheaf equivalence;
the new code supplies its structured torsor and target-map consequences.

[PrincipalSheafContext](../../SquareKit/PrincipalSheafContext.lean) connects
the old sheaf-context torsor to its principal sheaf on `D`, then to a torsor
over the terminal sheaf on `D`. Its torsor and quotient functors are fully
faithful, with explicit recovery of arrows. They preserve the regular torsor
and reflect trivializations. The concrete consumer is therefore the original
nontrivial torsor, transported through a proved bridge.

For the original-site constructions of #657 and #665, `structureOf_eq_elementStructure`
and `targetMap_slice` identify the structures used here with
`SheafifiedSliceTorsor.elementStructure` and the existing slice target map.
`sliceTorsorRecovery` and `sliceQuotientRecovery` whisker the existing #665
recovery units by the actual site-restriction functors. Their naturality,
projection and target equations retain the original display recovery. The
subcanonical case continues to use `SheafifiedSubcanonical`; this work does
not construct a second original-slice equivalence.

## Restriction and descent

Parameter restriction commutes strictly with forward site change, both on
torsors and on quotients. The identity and two-step equations use the actual
composite parameter map. The natural restriction comparisons consequently
have identity components.

[SheafSiteComparisonDescent](../../SquareKit/SheafSiteComparisonDescent.lean)
compares full descent categories using the existing effective family descent
equivalences. Its canonical descent square and recovery square are natural
isomorphisms. Local comparison maps identify the resulting object at each
chart with direct site restriction, commute with every compatible datum map,
and preserve every overlap transition. The refinement square includes an
arbitrary base map and a covering-family refinement, using
`SheafFamilyDescent.recoveryNaturality` and Mathlib's canonical descent/pull
comparison. Two successive refinements compare through their actual composite
using Mathlib's `pullFunctorCompIso` on both sites.

## Checked controls

The example module checks the following connections to the public comparison:

- The two chart fibers cover the terminal parameter on `D`; neither fiber
  covers alone, and their covering sieve is proper.
- A cross-chart overlap arrow joins distinct chart-labelled objects. The
  changed torsor obeys the original gluing equation along it, and the overlap
  transition is not the identity.
- The changed torsor admits no global trivialization. Its genuine quotient
  target distinguishes two explicit sections in an overlap fiber.
- Full proper-cover descent followed by site comparison and recovery retains
  this nontriviality, the actual target map and two distinct C4 stabilizers.
- Projected scale-by-two and scale-by-three maps are nonidentity parameter
  substitutions. Their successive restriction satisfies the site-comparison
  composition equation for the actual torsor and quotient. Their covering
  refinements also instantiate the two-step descent comparison on these
  objects.
- Either single chart fails density. With no charts, the same forward quotient
  restriction identifies the two distinct stabilizer arrows and is not
  faithful. Thus continuity and cocontinuity alone do not give the categorical
  equivalence claimed under the stronger hypotheses.

## Sources, setup and maintenance

The primary comparison reference is the Stacks Project,
[Section 7.29, Lemmas 7.29.1 and 7.29.3](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/039Z),
consulted 2026-11-23. Its comparison lemma and sliced comparison motivate the
construction. The checked implementation uses the pinned Mathlib
`Sites.DenseSubsite` APIs, `Functor.sheafEquiv`, induced topologies and
`Pseudofunctor.DescentData`. The Stacks statements are supporting mathematics;
the additional structured transport claims are proved in Lean here.

Working independently on each site would require a second principal-action
construction, independent quotient target recovery and a comparison of their
descent maps. The shared implementation instead adds a parameter-element
density lemma and transports the existing principal structures. Its costs are
the induced chart-site interface, the terminal-context bridge, the structured
arrow/target recovery proofs and the compatibility with Mathlib descent data.
These are the new maintenance surfaces, alongside the pinned dense-subsite
sheaf-equivalence API. Existing torsor definitions, local lifting proofs and
effective cover descent are reused.

A direct expansion of the chosen sheaf-equivalence counit exceeded the default
200,000-heartbeat limit and also an external 800,000-heartbeat diagnostic run.
Factoring through the named transport and `restrict_extend` boundaries brought
the torsor module back to default limits. The concrete and generic modules
retain no increased heartbeat or recursion setting. Failed attempts and exact
validation receipts remain in the external implementation/review evidence,
not as unfinished source obligations.

Broader presentation invariance remains with the programme in #666/#647;
smooth geometric realization remains #640. Associated bundles and extension
along a group homomorphism remain #669: preserving an existing equivariant
target map does not construct either operation. These are separate from this
finite site comparison. The one-universe
interface can be generalized if a consumer needs larger fibers or independent
hom universes; the present result makes no such claim.
