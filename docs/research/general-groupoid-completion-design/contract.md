## Concrete construction

Use the following right-action convention to make the unit covariant without hiding inverse labels. Write `g ≫ h` for first `g`, then `h`.

A bundle over `X : E` consists of a split sheaf family `P`, an anchor `a : extension P ⟶ G.objects`, and a right action. At a section stage, if `p` has anchor `a(p)` and `g : b → a(p)` is an actual arrow of `G`, then `p · g` has anchor `b`. Require naturality under restriction, the anchor equation, unit and associativity equations, local surjectivity of `extension P → X`, and principality:

`∀ p q in the same fiber, ∃! g : a(q) → a(p), p · g = q`.

Equivalently, the map from the action domain to `P ×_X P`, sending `(p,g)` to `(p,p·g)`, is an isomorphism. Choose one presentation and derive the other. Do not store both as independent obligations. Principality permits empty section fibers; local surjectivity supplies local existence. A globally inhabited fiber requirement would exclude the desired no-global-chart cases. Without local surjectivity the empty bundle would satisfy principality vacuously and break local generation.

The section operations are derived from `G`'s sheaf morphisms. Although `Operations.Laws` quantifies over generalized arrows from sheaf parameters, this causes no subcanonicality requirement: instantiate the laws on the arrow sheaf and the sheaves of composable pairs/triples to obtain morphism equations, then evaluate them on sections. Alternatively use sheafified representables. A helper layer for section-level composition and its restriction laws belongs in the general groupoid code, rather than an extra law field in every bundle.

Bundle morphisms are all natural maps of families preserving anchor and action. Every such map is invertible: locally choose a source point `p`; for a target point `q`, principality in the target gives the unique `g` with `f(p)·g=q`, and `p·g` is its preimage. Independence of `p` follows by principality, so these preimages glue by the source sheaf condition. Injectivity follows locally from uniqueness of the connector. This yields the groupoid structure. Defining arrows initially as equivariant family isomorphisms, as `SheafTorsor.Arrow` does, is also workable, provided this theorem establishes that no equivariant maps were excluded. It is a derived property, not an extra requirement on maps.

Substitution is `SplitSheaf.tySub` with precomposition of anchor/action data, preserving the current strict substitution API where possible. The local-surjectivity proof is the same sheaf-fiber pullback argument used in `SheafTorsor.reindex_isLocallySurjective` and `SheafCover.baseChangeCover_isLocallySurjective`.

For `m : X → G.objects`, construct the regular bundle `R(m)` whose fiber at `x` consists of arrows `r : b → m(x)`, anchored at `b`, with `r·g = g ≫ r`. Its local existence is witnessed by `id_m`, and its connector from `r` to `q` is `q ≫ r⁻¹`. For `f : m → n`, set `R(f)(r)=r ≫ f`. Evaluation of a bundle arrow `R(m) → R(n)` at `id_m` recovers `f : m → n`; equivariance says that every value is then `r ≫ f`. This proves a natural equivalence on the full hom types, hence unit full faithfulness and preservation of ineffective arrows. A proof identifying only endpoints or isomorphism classes would not establish this claim.

For an arbitrary bundle `P`, its total-space cover has a tautological section `p`. Over that cover, `R(a(p)) → P` sends `g` to `p·g`. Principality proves this is an isomorphism. This is the required singleton-cover local generation with an explicit local object and isomorphism. There is no supplied local-generation field.

## Descent and derivability obligations

Its `evaluationEquiv`, `evaluationIso`, `Map.descend`, and canonical recovery give the underlying object and arrow descent. `SheafTorsorDescent.lean` already builds group actions on that construction. The groupoid case adds a partial action whose domain depends on the anchor; it cannot be obtained merely by changing the type of a group label in that file.

Given a bundle descent datum, first forget to the display datum. Descend the anchor because the transition maps preserve it. There is already a generic effective-epimorphism map descent operation in `SheafActionQuotient.Descent.descendMap` (`SheafActionQuotientDescent.lean:22`), used to descend the old target map at line 92. Extract or reuse that generic operation at an appropriate dependency level. Descend the action by its compatible local values, or descend its sheaf map after pulling back the action-domain cover. The latter route requires proving the domain cover is a base change of the original cover. The anchor equation ensures the partial-action domains match.

Unit, associativity, and restriction equations are local equalities and follow from the evaluation comparisons. Local surjectivity follows by composing the original cover with local bundle covers. For principality, local connectors between two descended sections agree on overlaps by local uniqueness; glue them in `G.arrows`, recover their source and target by separatedness in `G.objects`, and verify the action equation locally. This is where the arrow sheaf hypothesis does substantive work. It cannot be replaced by an arbitrary arrow presheaf without a new construction.

For arrows, descend the underlying natural family map and verify anchor preservation/equivariance locally. Prove both hom recovery identities, then convert this to Mathlib's `toDescentData` equivalence. The existing `SheafTorsorStackDescent` and `SheafActionQuotientStackDescent` show the adapters and natural comparison obligations. A bare existence proof for glued objects is insufficient: `IsStack` includes prestackhood and the acceptance criterion retains all arrows.

Source prestackhood is derivable independently: for fixed `m,n : X → G.objects`, arrows are represented over `X` by the pullback of `G.arrows → G.objects × G.objects` along `(m,n)`. Maps to this sheaf descend along locally surjective maps by effective-epi descent, giving the hom sheaf. Another legitimate proof is `StackObjectPropertyDescent.isPrestack_of_fullyFaithful` after the bundle target has independently been proved a stack. The current action-presentable completion uses that second route. Neither route may assume source prestackhood as new primitive data.

`StackificationUniversal.universalEquivalence` at lines 3347–3369 requires pullbacks in the parameter category, componentwise fully faithful `η`, singleton local generation, and that the target `T` is a stack. Its actual forward functor is definitionally `StackLocalGeneration.restriction η T`, namely bicategorical precomposition, and its arrows are modifications. It does not itself require the completion `B` to be a stack. Therefore applying it alone does not discharge effective descent of the new completion; that must remain a separately proved theorem. It does provide the requested full universal property once the explicit unit has been checked.

## Alternative and decision boundary

Completion by cover-indexed descent data is mathematically valid. The Stacks Project defines the quotient of an algebraic-space groupoid as stackification of its section groupoid and recovers group-action quotients as a specialization; its hypotheses there are algebraic spaces over a base with the fppf topology, not a ready-made theorem for this Lean input ([Stacks Project, 78.20](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/044O)). Mathlib's inspected descent files define descent categories and stack predicates, including chosen-pullback descent data, but do not construct the required arbitrary completion in those files.

A cover-data implementation needs all cross-cover arrows, refinement equivalence, their composition, coherent reindexing, and descent of descent data. Storing `(cover, cocycle)` as objects and only maps on the same cover loses arrows. Quotienting objects to isomorphism classes loses isotropy. Quantifying over arbitrary cover domains also needs a fresh universe accounting. Principal bundles absorb refinement through actual sheaves and use the library's existing effective display descent. These are implementation and representation advantages, not an impossibility result against cover-data completion.

The smallest useful first implementation checkpoint is the principal-bundle object/arrow interface, regular unit, full hom recovery, and total-space trivialization. In parallel with statement review, test the discrete-groupoid unit and the action-groupoid inverse-label recovery. If those definitions cannot preserve the stated universes or the old action arrows without additional hypotheses, reconsider the representation before building stack descent. Then complete the effective-descent adapter and universal property, followed by the independent atlas consumer and coherence comparisons.
