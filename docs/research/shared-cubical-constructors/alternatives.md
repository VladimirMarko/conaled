## Evidence for retaining the native construction

`SumFilling.lean:12–64` and `CartesianDependentFilling.lean:123–175` implement the same algorithm: fill the first projection, form the second box over that actual section using its cap/face witness, fill the dependent component, and pair the outputs. Their uniformity proofs both use a subtype of sections satisfying the first box, apply congruence to equality of those witnessed sections, then use second-box reindexing and the second filler’s uniformity. This is stronger evidence for a common proof than mere matching theorem names. It also shows exactly what must survive extraction: the first section is computational input to the second fill.

The cap difference is real but local to box data. Original calls carry a `Cofibration` and fixed-false cap; Cartesian calls carry a `Subcontext` and a reindexed interval term `r`. Neither Σ implementation consumes De Morgan connection or reversal. Those operations enter other constructions, especially Π, and should not become mandatory fields of a Σ interface.

Both classifiers retain complete chosen local operations. Original `SmallFamilyGlue.lean:64` derives `decoded_code`; Cartesian `CartesianSmallFamilyClassification.lean:469–489` derives `decodeCode_record`, `code_decode`, and `localFamilyEquiv`. Cartesian lines 382–411 first establish exact fill compatibility, including `graph r` in the cap comparison. A common classifier that records only underlying-family equivalence would therefore weaken existing clients. `Examples/CartesianSmallFamilyClassification.lean:47–55` explicitly exercises a variable cap; lines 72–97 retain a proper endpoint face and the selected record on that face.

## Three viable factorizations

1. **Retained native modules with explicit comparison theorems.** This gives immediate recovery and least migration. `CartesianRestriction.sigmaComparison` at line 223 already retains both components, with the dependent family pulled through `totalComparison.inv`. Its source and target are exact. It is a family isomorphism, not a filler-preservation theorem. This option remains useful even after extracting shared proofs.

2. **Shared represented-chart classification.** Factor generic chart evaluation and reconstruction from the model-specific box/filler package. The minimal mathematical input should describe a small base category, representables and their generic elements, substitution-stable local structured families, and their concrete decoding/reconstruction. Prove the classifier round trips from those ingredients; do not make the round trips or sought selected-operation preservation mandatory fields. `LargeFamilyClassification.lean:73–120` already demonstrates a valuable construction: small fiber representatives plus equivalences, with maps derived by conjugation, become a code through lawful chart fillers. A cross-model abstraction must preserve its universe distinction: small fibers over an arbitrary larger context are not a small context. The Cartesian baseline's raised-small-context classifier is not already a specialization of this larger-context contract.

3. **Shared dependent filling followed by optional bridges.** State box shape as model-dependent parameters, with projection, dependent second-box construction, pairing and substitution laws. Derive the first-then-second operation and its lawfulness. The builder proves structural box facts; callers give their existing lawful fillers. A later bridge can preserve those elementary operations and the two input fillers and derive preservation of the constructed Σ filler. This avoids assuming the desired Σ preservation as a field. For a bridge `T`, its second-stage obligation is over the image of the *selected first filler*, with explicit comprehension comparison; preservation over an unrelated first section is insufficient. This route separates capability reuse from interpretation between distinct models.

The smallest useful extraction is (3), optionally sharing generic-evaluation lemmas from (2). A giant record covering Π, Glue, univalence and classifier universes is not justified by this slice. Its absence is not a mathematical impossibility claim.

## A broader primary alternative

Cavallo, Mörtberg and Swan's [Unifying Cubical Models of Univalent Type Theory](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol152-csl2020/LIPIcs.CSL.2020.14/LIPIcs.CSL.2020.14.pdf), Definition 2, Sections 2.3.1–2.3.2 and Theorem 15, gives a relevant alternative: weak composition includes a cap path in place of strict cap equality. Diagonal cofibrations yield the Cartesian comparison; connections and reversal yield the CCHM comparison. Its dependent-sum proof supplies shared mathematical substance beyond chart classification. This is evidence against treating native cap differences as a universal barrier. It does not establish exact recovery of this repository's selected filling records. Adopting it here would require constructing adapters and proving which original selections are recovered, or retaining explicit cap-path witnesses under a weaker comparison. I recommend keeping this as the broader alternative when strict selected-operation recovery fails, rather than silently weakening the current contract. No model search or Agda verification was repeated.

## Adjoint-square reuse

`AdjointSquareTransport.curry`, `curry_paste`, and `uncurry_paste` transport an actual supplied arrow using a square and its mate. They need no thinness, properness or projection-formula hypothesis. They are directly reusable if the bounded comparison is expressed as transport of a dependent-function transpose or its corresponding adjunction. Their composition endpoints are explicit: a map from `G'.obj (G.obj X)` to `R''.obj (H'.obj (H.obj Y))`, with successive mate components at `Y` and `H.obj Y`; `curry_paste` identifies this with transpose through the pasted square.

For the chosen Σ algorithm, the missing data is instead the dependent box and cap/face structure, the first filler selection, and the second filler over its selected first section. An adjunction on underlying family categories does not produce these laws. One may use the dual adjunction route for the underlying dependent-sum comparison, but should first compare it with the existing elementwise `sigmaComparison`, which is already an isomorphism retaining the coefficient. Repackaging that isomorphism as a mate provides coherence infrastructure, not a proof of filling preservation. #570 supplies a good discipline: derive compatibility of the composite from compatibility of the components; reuse its mates engine when an actual transpose is involved.

Pinned Felix [Comma/Homomorphism](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/conal/felix/6f36d28097db7ea5f5df3263db3e8be08f74e490/src/Felix/Construct/Comma/Homomorphism.agda) separates projections and category preservation. [MakeLawful](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/conal/felix/6f36d28097db7ea5f5df3263db3e8be08f74e490/src/Felix/MakeLawful.agda), lines 30–87, transports laws for induced equivalence under explicit preservation hypotheses. This supports separating operations, comparison and laws here; it does not supply cubical caps or exact native filler recovery. `MakeLawful.agda` is at `src/Felix/`, not under `Construct/Comma/`.

## Discriminating acceptance test and remaining limits

Instantiate any shared Σ construction twice and prove equality with each native filling record, including a dependent second component and the Cartesian variable-cap case. Prove the native classifier recovers each resulting selected record. Include a proper face so that full-boundary uniqueness cannot hide a changed fill. A concrete distinct-selection control would strengthen the currently conditional `choices_separated` example, but generic whole-record equalities already quantify over selections.

For a bridge, compose two actual routes with their comprehension maps and show that transporting the selected pair and its dependent witness along the composite equals successive transport. A proof merely about vertex observations or family objects does not meet this condition. The existing `no_interval_retraction` rejects an endpoint-preserving natural map from the restricted De Morgan represented interval to the Cartesian interval, via reversal. It rules out that particular interval identification; it does not rule out common weak composition, a different interval interpretation, or a restricted bridge with explicit hypotheses.

No established defect in the native implementation was found. The unresolved obligations are exact shared-specialization recovery, the large-context Cartesian classifier extension, and a bridge preserving actual box data and selections. Assign the first to this bounded decision, keep the Cartesian admission work with #605 and exact decoder-filler questions with #607, and retain broader interpretation work under #235/#289. These are separate mathematical obligations, not objections based on current adapter cost.
