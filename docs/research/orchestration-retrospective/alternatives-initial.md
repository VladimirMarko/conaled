# Alternatives, selection bias and neglected integration

Recommendation: retain the bounded constructions and native interfaces, but stop using coverage of more traditions as the main evidence of architectural progress. The next assessment should test a missing end-to-end contract or a repeated author obligation that can defeat the current approach. The record supports substantial mathematical progress and deliberate corrections to selection bias. It does not establish a generally superior architecture, economical large-scale proof reuse, or completion of the source-to-smooth-model programme.

This independent assessment uses the pinned checkout c4cbfdea38817f64760fb03319a94d25422dc65a and the supplied current issue snapshot. I read no other assessor's report and had no prior participation in these developments. I inspected reports and selected Lean declarations, without building, changing repository sources, creating issues, or contacting other sessions. Claims of prior acceptance are the records' claims, not new validation. External literature was not independently re-reviewed; the conclusions concern the local treatment of alternatives.

## What the whole arc changed

The earlier author/productivity work already showed restraint. #249 retained both direct and shared interfaces and specified later counterbalanced, replicated trials rather than treating successful DList examples as an optimal architecture (`docs/research/toolkit-author-effectiveness.md:133,165`). #293 found both manual and ledger-assisted authors could complete the selected contracts. The manual route even exposed a weaker observation-specific law; its separating model shows why an inventory of proof dependencies is not automatically discovery of weakest useful requirements (`docs/research/proof-requirement-author-trial.md:137,173`). These are results against obligatory framework adoption, not failed projects.

The institutions/shared-meaning work made a useful distinction: computational realization and proof translation must agree through the same evidence comparison; packaging two unrelated correctness squares is insufficient. Its instantiated signature connections do not constitute full institution infrastructure (`docs/research/shared-meaning-experiment-results.md:10`, `docs/research/progressive-institution-capabilities.md:9`). The subsequent architecture comparison allowed native reuse to win: #361 needed no new external solution interface; #358's native author reused global laws and contained the repair as well as the shared author (`docs/research/architecture-scaling-study/results.md:59`).

There was an actual selection-policy defect. The repository itself records that requiring a second existing consumer before a discovery experiment favored capabilities already present in the library (`docs/research/semantic-traditions-next-work.md:42`). Later work corrected it in material ways:

- Probability got an actual function-space/random-element model and joint-law discriminator, then representation-independent randomized continuation substitution (#370/#391). This went beyond another affine adapter. The inspected `two_stage_substitution` requires standard Borel seed/noise spaces, s-finite noise measures and admitted QBS morphisms, and preserves equality of whole resulting laws (`Applications/Probability/SamplerSubstitution.lean:86`). It does not equate random seeds or establish arbitrary conditioning/recursive probability.
- SDT progressed from paper comparisons and a prerequisite graph to the complete bounded assembly provider (#371/#385–#389). `solver` uses constructed cut selection/completeness; `solver_external_solution` compares full selected certificate states; `contextualSolver` genuinely varies capacity over an assembly context (`Examples/InternalCertificateSolver.lean:9,37,50`). The report excludes nonuniform external states and separates tracker existence from executable machine adequacy (`docs/research/internal-selected-certificate-solver.md:13,125,144`). Calling this still merely scheduled would be wrong.
- Nominal binding, modal access, state/exception policies, relational transport and wiring gained checked extensions rather than remaining paper alternatives. Their fourth-wave boundaries are specific: the indexed relation is still graph-like, modal primitives are shallow functions over restricted carriers, and changing unequal fibers preserves a specified selection rather than every prior index (`docs/research/semantic-traditions-fourth-wave.md:76,79`).
- Truth-oriented Horn support and classical control were promoted after being passively deferred. Horn support is genuinely Prop-valued and quantifies over permitted future bases; it does not demand proof traces in its core (`SquareKit/HornSupport.lean:42`). Its persistence fixes the permitted basis, so it does not prove invariance under changing the ambient policy. A separate labelled layer supplies selected derivations. This is evidence against the claim that every alternative was forced to preserve maximal witnesses.
- Games received a finite serial interaction model with actual channel matching/hiding, plus an intentionally coarser constant-return quotient (#390). It is neither an implementation of general AJM games nor a rejection of games. The native/strategy theorem uses arbitrary adaptive environments within the finite query family (`Applications/Interaction/StrategyQueries.lean:47,74`); the quotient explicitly separates observations that descend from histories/adaptive observers that do not (`docs/research/finite-interaction-strategies.md:132`).

The learned architecture is observation-specific: support, selected derivations, law equality, full service histories, hidden channel plays, and internal tracked maps have different legitimate contracts. No largest preservation record subsumes them without cost. This is a substantive revision of the earlier tendency to use retained selected witnesses as the universal test.

## Where selection bias remains

**Established sampling limitation:** the tests heavily favor finite generated syntax, small controlled families, selected outputs and changes expressly designed for a bounded theorem. Fourth- and fifth-wave reports acknowledge this (`docs/research/semantic-traditions-fourth-wave.md:143`, `docs/research/semantic-traditions-fifth-wave.md:91`). A negative control proves a distinction inside its chosen contract; it does not show the contract is representative of the library's future consumers.

The control tradition illustrates the distinction. Failure of the delivery pole to recover the first answer is evidence against using adequacy to prove a stronger selected-search contract. It is not evidence that a truth/realizability client should carry that stronger observation. Likewise, a coarse relation forgetting a chosen steady state is correct when its consumer asks only for a boundary relation. The later Horn and games constructions are counterevidence to a blanket accusation of witness bias; they do not erase the sample imbalance.

**Plausible remaining risk:** the default trigger “when a consumer needs X” can become a circular discovery gate if consumers are drawn mainly from incumbent-friendly code. Matching logic, local universes, quantitative dependent syntax, higher-order games and probability-sheaf/internal-valuation variants remain eligible but mostly have residual owners and conditional re-entry clauses (`docs/research/architecture-scaling-study/disposition.md:197`; `docs/research/semantic-traditions-coverage.md:265`). This is a reason to preserve a small external capability search, not evidence that any one of those frameworks should be adopted. Absence of an immediate adapter should remain irrelevant to discovery eligibility.

**Unknown:** whether a different foundation removes more author effort over a large reusable library. #358 was a real fresh-author comparison, and both authors succeeded. But depth, primitives, settings and interactions varied together; one author per arm, configuration gaps, anticipated later tasks and unmeasured design/setup work prevent a causal scaling conclusion (`docs/research/dependent-operation-scaling.md:142,183,233`). Native authors were correctly allowed to factor reusable proofs. A future comparison that forces native repetition would manufacture a benefit for the shared interface.

The samples also limit substantive generality, beyond measurement noise: #358 reconstructs the chosen tail from a runtime value rather than retaining arbitrary incoming dependent witnesses (`docs/research/dependent-operation-scaling.md:216`). #376's concrete swap uses a symmetric sum, so the example cannot reveal an uncompensated port-swap error even though the generic theorem is broader (`docs/research/semantic-traditions-third-wave.md`, wiring discussion). These are precise missing discriminators, not proof defects.

## Small discriminating next assessments

1. **Audit one missing complete consumer contract before commissioning another tradition slice.** Trace an unchanged source derivation through model admission, selected higher witness, smooth interpretation and ordinary caller, marking each existing theorem and the first missing edge. Respect #107/#108's pause and independence gates; this can be a read-only gap assessment under #100/#289/#290. A collection of adjacent positive examples fails this test. A precise demonstrated incompatibility is an acceptable result.
2. **Choose the next author comparison around a recurring obligation the present helpers do not remove.** Candidates include arbitrary incoming dependent payloads or a genuinely new model/constructor interaction, not merely larger constants. Freeze matching observations, let native authors use all available general laws, separate setup/model-construction from repeated authoring, and vary one scaling dimension or replicate. Include a deliberately coarser legitimate observation where applicable. Success means measured evidence of saved work or a clear explanation of why the native route remains preferable, not more theorem declarations.
3. **Review the dormant-trigger ledger against external capabilities.** For a small sample, ask whether the absence of a local consumer is a real scope choice or a consequence of the current representation. Require an independently stated capability and a test the incumbent could fail; allow an alternative to avoid an incumbent adapter. Also reconcile #289 and wave summaries with the accepted SDT/sampler results. This assessment should not reopen every closed bounded ticket or create a new universal-framework mandate.

The strongest case for retaining the current direction is that these waves repeatedly discovered useful existing laws, accepted native success, constructed alternatives on independent assumptions, and kept failure controls that exposed overstrong claims. The strongest case for changing the evaluation strategy is that this now-rich collection of bounded successes still leaves the project's principal reuse and integration promises undecided.
