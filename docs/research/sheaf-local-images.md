# Sheaf-local existential images

This experimental development implements
[#51](https://github.com/VladimirMarko/conaled/issues/51),
[#52](https://github.com/VladimirMarko/conaled/issues/52),
[#53](https://github.com/VladimirMarko/conaled/issues/53),
[#54](https://github.com/VladimirMarko/conaled/issues/54),
[#55](https://github.com/VladimirMarko/conaled/issues/55),
[#56](https://github.com/VladimirMarko/conaled/issues/56),
[#57](https://github.com/VladimirMarko/conaled/issues/57),
[#58](https://github.com/VladimirMarko/conaled/issues/58),
and [#59](https://github.com/VladimirMarko/conaled/issues/59) within the
internal-language workstream. It supplies local existential images, their
Frobenius and Beck–Chevalley laws, sheaf contexts, terms and closed formula
predicates, regular source-rule soundness, sheaf function spaces, dependent
context extension, and finite applications.
It does not complete #36.

## Public interface and caller obligations

[SquareKit/SheafImage.lean](../../SquareKit/SheafImage.lean) uses Mathlib's
`Sheaf`, `Subfunctor.image`, `Subfunctor.preimage`, and `Subfunctor.sheafify`.
`localImage Q f` is the sheaf closure of the pointwise image. The indexing
objects, morphisms, and sections have independent universes. Callers supply
a Grothendieck topology, type-valued sheaves, a morphism, and an ordinary
subfunctor predicate. The predicate Q need not itself be a sheaf.

`mem_localImage` characterizes membership by a covering sieve;
`witness_arrow` expands its arrows as restrictions of the target section with
actual source witnesses satisfying Q. These witnesses need not agree on
overlaps. `localImage_isSheaf` discharges the sheaf obligation from the target
sheaf, and `restrict` exposes restriction stability.

`adjunction` says `localImage Q f ≤ R ↔ Q ≤ R.preimage f.hom`, provided R's
subfunctor is a sheaf. `preimage_isSheaf` establishes closure of the reindexed
predicate. `monotone` derives monotonicity from this adjunction. An arbitrary
subfunctor R cannot replace a closed R: the finite example's pointwise image
contains the image of every source section but fails to contain a target
section that lies in the image locally.

`frobenius Q f R hR` proves

```lean
localImage (Q ⊓ R.preimage f.hom) f = localImage Q f ⊓ R
```

Here Q remains an arbitrary subfunctor, and `hR` proves that R's underlying
functor is a sheaf. This is the new caller obligation. `inf_isSheaf` proves
closure of intersection on an ambient sheaf; `localImage_inf_isSheaf` exposes
it for the right-hand side. The forward inclusion uses monotonicity and the
closed-target adjunction. For the reverse inclusion, restrict the known R
section along the covering sieve and attach it to each local Q witness.
No compatibility or choice of a global source witness is required.

For Q = top, `top_eq_range` identifies the predicate with Mathlib's chosen
sheaf image predicate. `imageEquiv` identifies sections with those of
`Sheaf.image`; its naturality, inclusion, and factor lemmas preserve the chosen
image inclusion and source factor. There is no second image object or
sheafification implementation.

The ordinary graph, relation, interval, and presheaf interfaces are unchanged.
The reusable local-image module imports no internal syntax. The finite
consumer imports the existing presheaf and direct clients and uses a direct
graph calculation beside the syntax adapter, without new global notation or
model instances. Context isomorphisms occur only in this opt-in comparison;
ordinary clients acquire no environment representation or conversion.

## Finite counterexample

[Examples/SheafImage.lean](../../Examples/SheafImage.lean) constructs the diamond
W → U → X and W → V → X, with the two composites equal. At X, precisely the
maximal sieve and the sieve generated by U and V cover. At every other object
only the maximal sieve covers. `topology` proves maximality, pullback stability,
and transitivity; `cover_generated`, `covering_X`, and `covering_elsewhere`
verify the claimed coverage.

The sheaf E has sections Empty at X, Unit at U and V, and Bool at W. The two
proper restrictions select false and true. `no_compatible` proves that any
family on any cover of X is incompatible. Thus the sheaf condition at X is
vacuous, with its premise explicitly ruled out; it does not assert that the
incompatible pair glues. At the other objects the maximal-sieve theorem supplies
the sheaf condition. The constant Unit presheaf is constructed as a sheaf and
proved terminal.

For E → 1, `locally_witnessed` uses the reusable membership and witness
characterizations. It supplies Unit witnesses over U and V and a Bool witness
over W. `localImage_top` proves the local image is the top predicate at every
object. `no_pointwise_witness` rejects a witness for the same terminal section
at X. `imageSection` and `imageSection_inclusion` exhibit it through Mathlib's
image interface. `pointwise_not_sheaf` uses the general adjunction to show
exactly why the unclosed image cannot serve as a closed target predicate.

`selective` is the terminal predicate inhabited exactly at W and U.
Restriction preserves its support. `selective_isSheaf` uses the maximal covers
away from X; at X every covering sieve contains V, where this predicate is
empty. `selective_image` applies the public Frobenius theorem and
`localImage_top` to compute its constrained image as `selective` itself.
The checked U membership and V/X nonmembership follow from that equality.
The top specialization `frobenius_top_at_X` retains local truth and the absence
of a pointwise witness for the same map.

For the unclosed pointwise image R, `pointwise_preimage_top` shows that every
source section satisfies the pulled-back condition. `unclosed_frobenius_at_X`
then proves the proposed equation's left side at X and refutes its right side;
`unclosed_frobenius_fails` gives inequality of subfunctors. This R fails the
closure assumption by `pointwise_not_sheaf`, so the example does not contradict
the general theorem.

The underlying-presheaf adapter interprets an existential reflexive equality
in empty context. `singletonIso`, `emptyIso`, and `projection_comparison`
identify its context projection with E → 1. `formula_image_comparison`
transports the interpreted formula to the pointwise image of that map.
`presheaf_exists_false`, `evaluated_exists_false`, and `sheaf_exists_true`
therefore compare the same existence claim. Objectwise evaluation of sheaves
does not in general preserve existential images. This does not refute the
presheaf evaluation theorem, either existing soundness theorem, or a source
rule. No reflection theorem or full sheaf-language soundness follows.

## Substitution along sheaf pullbacks

[SquareKit/SheafBaseChange.lean](../../SquareKit/SheafBaseChange.lean) proves
`beckChevalley` for a Mathlib `IsPullback g' f' f g` square:

```text
A' --g'--> A
 |         |
f'         f
 |         |
v          v
B' --g---> B
```

For any subfunctor Q on A, the equality is

```lean
(localImage Q f).preimage g.hom = localImage (Q.preimage g'.hom) f'
```

Callers supply the actual categorical pullback proof. `sections_isPullback`
maps it through `sheafToPresheaf` and evaluation, using Mathlib's limit
preservation instances. It introduces no sectionwise lifting assumption.
On each arrow of a witnessing covering sieve, the sectionwise pullback pairs
a Q witness with the restricted B' section. The reverse inclusion projects
that witness and uses commutativity and naturality. Neither direction asks
local witnesses to agree on overlaps. `beckChevalley_isSheaf` proves closure
of both predicates using existing image and inverse-image closure; it does
not even need the square to be a pullback. All three universes remain
independent, and Q need not be closed.

[Examples/SheafBaseChange.lean](../../Examples/SheafBaseChange.lean) constructs
the constant Bool sheaf D. Every cover contains an arrow from W, and family
compatibility forces the Bool values to coincide there; `bool_isSheaf` proves
the gluing and uniqueness obligations. Its map `base : D → 1` identifies
false and true at X (`base_not_injective`). Mathlib's chosen pullback of
E → 1 along this map is a sheaf by the existing sheaf limit machinery.
`productSections` identifies its sections with E sections paired with Bool;
the projection and naturality theorems connect this explicit representation
to the categorical square.

`baseChanged_top` uses the general Beck–Chevalley theorem and the original
top image calculation. Hence every Bool section at X belongs locally to the
base-changed image (`locally_at_X`). `no_pointwise_at_X` projects any alleged
global witness to the forbidden original E section and reuses
`no_pointwise_witness`. For the source predicate pulled back from `selective`
along E → 1, `baseChanged_selective` combines Beck–Chevalley with the earlier
Frobenius computation. Its checked consequences admit both Bool values at U
and exclude both at V and X. These computations use the public laws rather
than proving a separate finite version of local existential image.

The new reusable module has no internal-syntax dependency. The example keeps
an ordinary graph calculation beside the sheaf constructions. Existing graph,
relation, interval and presheaf clients gain no new arguments or obligations.

## Contexts and terms from the existing syntax

[SquareKit/Internal/SheafContexts.lean](../../SquareKit/Internal/SheafContexts.lean)
takes the existing `Presheaf.Model` and proofs that each sort carrier is a
sheaf. It retains that adapter's universes: sorts in `u`, sections in
`max u w`, and the small indexing category in `v`. `context_isSheaf` glues an
environment one variable at a time using the corresponding carrier's sheaf
condition. The proof includes the empty context; it never asks every sort
for an inhabitant. `context` packages the same presheaf environment functor,
and `sort` packages each given carrier.

`term`, `substitution`, and `projection` lift the existing natural
transformations without changing their underlying maps. Substitution identity,
composition, term substitution, and projection compatibility reuse the
presheaf equations. These constructions require no assumptions about relation
predicates. When relations are interpreted as sheaf predicates, their closure
will be an additional obligation.

For `σ : Sub F Γ Δ`, the interpreted substitution goes from the Δ context to
the Γ context. `context_pullback` proves that its lift under a new sort and
the two context projections form a categorical pullback. Given a cone, its
mediating environment takes the new variable from the extended Γ component
and its tail from the Δ component. The cone equation proves the lifted
substitution recovers the former; the head and tail projections determine
uniqueness. `exists_substitution` applies the general sheaf Beck–Chevalley law
to this square for arbitrary predicates on the extended Γ context.

[Examples/SheafContexts.lean](../../Examples/SheafContexts.lean) interprets two
sorts using the existing E and Bool sheaves and gives Bool negation as a
natural primitive. `emptyIso` and `empty_unique` identify the empty context
with the terminal sheaf, including its section at X. `singletonIso` identifies
the one-variable contexts with their sort sheaves: `e_at_X_empty` checks the
E case, while `boolEnv_distinct` separates the two Bool environments.
`extendedIso` identifies the E/Bool context with the earlier chosen pullback;
`extendedSections_apply` computes its product representation as head E value
and tail Bool value. The projection, lifted substitution, and bottom-map
comparisons connect every side to the earlier finite square.

`extended_top` uses the contextual substitution theorem. Hence both Bool
environments at X satisfy local existence, while `no_pointwise_at_X` reuses
the original obstruction to any E section there. For the W/U selective
predicate, `selected_image` uses Frobenius and `extended_selected` uses
contextual Beck–Chevalley; the U/V/X statements retain the expected support.
`neg_substitution` instantiates term substitution, and `twice_neg_value` uses
it to check that substituting negation into negation returns the original Bool.

The adapter is opt-in and introduces no new model hierarchy or syntax.
Its callers supply a presheaf interpretation and carrier sheaf proofs; context
sheafness and pullbacks are proved results. The syntax-free image/base-change
modules do not import the adapter. The mixed-style example retains an ordinary
graph calculation without new model arguments or setup for that calculation.

## Closed formula predicates and substitution

[SquareKit/Internal/SheafFormula.lean](../../SquareKit/Internal/SheafFormula.lean)
interprets the existing four regular constructors. Atoms and equality reuse
`Presheaf.Model.atom` and `equality`; conjunction is intersection, and
existence is `localImage` along the sheaf context projection. The interpretation
uses the existing presheaf model and carrier sheaf proofs, without new syntax
or a model hierarchy. To obtain `formula_isSheaf`, callers additionally prove
that each binary relation subfunctor is a sheaf.

`pairIso` identifies the existing relation product with a two-variable
context, so `product_isSheaf` follows from context sheafness. `pairTerms`
interprets the pair of arguments. Atomic closure is inverse-image closure of
the relation along this morphism. `equality_isSheaf` uses separation of the
sort carrier: two term values agreeing on a covering sieve are equal.
Conjunction and existential closure use the earlier intersection and local-image
results. Formula closure and substitution are proved, never assumed as model
fields. The adapter retains separate sort, section and indexing universes.

`formula_substitution` covers every formula and syntactic substitution.
Its atomic and equality cases reuse the existing presheaf interpretation
results; the conjunction case uses preimage of intersection. Under an
existential, the recursive equation uses the lifted substitution and
`exists_substitution` supplies contextual Beck–Chevalley. This proof does not
use objectwise evaluation to interpret a sheaf existential. `formula_restrict`
exposes restriction stability; `Valid` is predicate inclusion, separate from
`Derives`. The soundness theorem is supplied separately in
`SquareKit/Internal/SheafSoundness.lean`.

[Examples/SheafFormula.lean](../../Examples/SheafFormula.lean) extends the
E/Bool example with `selectedEq`: equality of Bool arguments together with
the earlier W/U support. Its restriction proof uses constant Bool maps and
support restriction; its closure proof obtains equal arguments on the
mandatory W arrow and uses the previously proved support closure. The
relation therefore tests both arguments and site support.

`existsE_image` connects the interpreted `∃ e, e = e` with the same local image
and context projection used earlier. `existsE_at_X` holds at the retained empty
environment, while `presheaf_existsE_false` and `evaluated_existsE_false`
refute the existing pointwise interpretation and evaluation of that very source
formula. The comparison uses the earlier context-to-E/terminal isomorphisms
through `context_top`, and reuses the original no-pointwise-witness result.

`combined` is `∃ e, e = e ∧ R(b,b)`. `combined_predicate` computes it using
Frobenius and the context top-image theorem, giving U membership and V/X
nonmembership. `unequalAtom_false` rejects `R(b,not b)` for every Bool and
site object. `combined_negation` specializes uniform formula substitution
under this existential and atom; the U membership and X nonmembership
consequences rewrite by that theorem and the checked negated environment.

Existing ordinary clients retain their interfaces, and the mixed graph example
continues alongside formula interpretation. The syntax-free sheaf modules have
no dependency on this adapter. Relation closure is a new obligation only for
callers who need closed formula predicates; contexts and terms still require
only carrier sheaf proofs.

## Source-rule soundness and Frobenius

[SquareKit/Internal/SheafSoundness.lean](../../SquareKit/Internal/SheafSoundness.lean)
proves `sound`: every current `Derives p q` gives `Valid M hM p q`, assuming
the carrier and relation sheaf proofs already used for formula closure.
The theorem covers identity, cut, conjunction rules, equality reflexivity,
symmetry, transitivity and unary congruence, existential introduction,
arbitrary syntactic substitution, and both existential-elimination rules.
There are no new source rules or model fields.

`formula_shift` identifies weakening with inverse image along projection;
`formula_instantiate` identifies instantiation with substitution of the
interpreted term. Introduction uses that actual term as a pointwise witness
and includes its image into the local image. Arbitrary source substitution
uses uniform formula substitution. Elimination uses the local-image adjunction
against the closed conclusion predicate. For `elimWith`, Frobenius first moves
the closed ambient hypothesis inside the existential. Formula closure
discharges these obligations. No local E witnesses are glued or turned into
a global source section.

`frobenius_valid` applies soundness to the existing `Frobenius.forward` and
`backward` derivations and takes both inclusions. The separate
`frobenius_semantic_comparison` uses weakening as preimage and conjunction as
intersection to identify the result with syntax-free sheaf Frobenius.
Source derivability, semantic inclusion and this comparison remain distinct.

[Examples/SheafSoundness.lean](../../Examples/SheafSoundness.lean) uses a source
`elim` derivation to infer `b=b` from local E-reflexivity existence at X.
`local_premise` and `no_global_premise` check that the antecedent holds locally
without any pointwise E witness. `frobenius_at_X` applies the source forward
derivation, which uses `elimWith`, with the inhabited condition `b=b`.
The selective source instance then connects to the earlier combined-formula
calculation; its U/V/X consequences retain the W/U support. Introduction uses
the actual Bool negation term, and a `Derives.subst` instance substitutes
negation into that introduction derivation. Both finite consequences use the
public sheaf soundness theorem.

This completes the proof-to-sheaf-model path for the present regular syntax:
finite typed contexts, unary primitive functions, binary relation atoms,
equality, conjunction and existence, with the listed sequent rules. It proves
neither completeness nor preservation of local existential truth by ordinary
objectwise evaluation. The finite E example still refutes that preservation.
There is no implication, universal quantifier, dependent-family interpretation,
SDG scalar or smooth site, higher identity, or cohesive structure in this
fragment. Ordinary clients and the syntax-free sheaf modules gain no dependency
on the source adapter; the mixed graph consumer remains unchanged.

## Ordinary closed relation composition

[SquareKit/SheafRelation.lean](../../SquareKit/SheafRelation.lean) supplies an
ordinary interface independent of source syntax. `Relation A B` is a subfunctor
of a product sheaf with explicit pair sections. `productIso` compares that
representation to Mathlib's chosen categorical product, preserving both
projections. The explicit product's sheaf proof glues its two components using
the given sheaves. Objects, morphisms and sections retain independent universes.

`comp R S` applies R first, S second. On the triple product represented as
`(intermediate, (source, target))`, `joined` intersects the inverse images of
R and S along `firstPair` and `secondPair`. Composition is the existing
`localImage` along `endpoints`. The operation and `comp_isSheaf` require no
closure premise on R or S. `mem_comp` and `witness_comp` characterize a covering
sieve on which each arrow admits an intermediate section satisfying the two
restricted relations. These intermediates need not agree on overlaps.

The optional [SheafAssociativity adapter](../../SquareKit/Internal/SheafAssociativity.lean)
interprets the existing four-sort source signature at any four type-valued
sheaves on a small indexing category. `source_valid` applies soundness to
unchanged `Associativity.forward` and `backward`, assuming all three input
relations are closed. The independent `left_comparison` and `right_comparison`
identify source predicates with the two ordinary composition parenthesizations.
They compare witnessing sieves, using the existing presheaf context-image
characterization only inside each local arrow. They never infer a global
witness from a covering sieve. `endpointIso` and the predicate-comparison
theorems express the endpoint environment/product comparison naturally.
`associative` combines source validity with both comparisons. Ordinary callers
of composition need no syntax model; callers of this optional associativity
theorem provide the three relation closure proofs, and its implementation
constructs the source interpretation.

[Examples/SheafRelations.lean](../../Examples/SheafRelations.lean) uses the chain
1 → E → Bool → Bool. With top relations, `both_at_X` establishes both local
parenthesizations at every Bool endpoint through the public associativity
transport. `no_intermediate_pair_at_X` refutes a pointwise pair of intermediate
sections, reusing the original no-E-witness proof. Hence evaluating this local
composition at X cannot be equated with pointwise Set relation composition.

For the selective chain, the final Bool relation is the earlier equal-arguments
relation supported at W/U. Its intermediate Bool is chosen equal to the target
Bool in `selected_composition`; the equality requirement is not discarded.
`selected_chain` uses transported associativity and the prior local E-image
calculation to compute exactly W/U support. U membership and V/X nonmembership
hold for every Bool endpoint. All relation-closure hypotheses are discharged.
The ordinary graph consumer, Set/presheaf associativity and historical examples
remain unchanged.

## Statement review and retained cases

The universal claims quantify over all sections and restrictions, with no
chosen global lift or compatible-witness premise. The sheaf assumptions occur
on the ambient sheaves and, for the adjunction and Frobenius, explicitly on R. The proof of
preimage closure uses naturality to compare restriction sieves. The image
comparison preserves the actual target section, not just the cardinality of
its fiber. These claims are exercised by the nontrivial finite site, including
reindexing a closed predicate and restricting local membership to U.

The finite construction checks both sources of potential vacuity separately:
compatible families at X cannot exist, but local image witnesses do exist.
The expected results follow from the independently specified Empty/Unit/Bool
sections and the unequal Bool restrictions. The context comparison uses the
existing image and evaluation theorems, rather than an unrelated pair of
True/False propositions. All declarations are imported by the public entrypoint
and included in the fresh-source build and transitive axiom audit.

During development, explicit `Env.extend` and reducible names for the concrete
sheaves avoided implicit-lambda and rewrite transparency failures. No global
elaborator settings, notation, instances on existing types, or heartbeat
increases were needed. The full check also recompiles the existing clients;
this is a compatibility check, not a claim of a measured speedup.

## Relation to source logic and the next slice

[#50](https://github.com/VladimirMarko/conaled/issues/50) proves source
Frobenius and transports it through the Set and presheaf soundness theorems.
In `SquareKit/Internal/PresheafFrobenius.lean`, conjunction is subfunctor
intersection and weakening is preimage along a context projection. The new
sheaf law has the same algebraic shape, with local existential image replacing
pointwise image. Its proof is a semantic theorem about arbitrary sheaf
morphisms. The closed formula adapter uses this law; existing source
derivability or presheaf validity does not supply sheaf soundness.

Beck–Chevalley now supplies the semantic law for substituting into an
existential predicate when context extension gives a pullback square. By
itself this law establishes neither a formula interpreter nor soundness.
The finite counterexample to objectwise preservation of existential truth
continues to apply.

The context construction supplies those actual pullback squares, and the
formula interpreter uses them in its uniform substitution theorem. Regular
source soundness now provides the proof-to-model link.

The ordinary relational consumer now completes the present regular-logic
comparison. More finite regular examples would not address the remaining
dependent-model gap. `Dependent.lean` still provides a
shallow split-family model over types, not dependent sheaf semantics, and no
smooth site or scalar has been constructed.

[SquareKit/SheafFunction.lean](../../SquareKit/SheafFunction.lean) now constructs
function spaces using the pinned Mathlib sheaf instance. For `C : Type u` with
`Category.{v} C`, its sections live in `Type (max u v)`. The imports
`Sites/LeftExact.lean` and `Monoidal/Closed/FunctorToTypes.lean` discharge
`HasSheafify J (Type (max u v))` and presheaf monoidal closedness; the former's
cover-smallness obligations are satisfied at this universe. Cartesian monoidal
structure comes from `Sites/CartesianMonoidal.lean`, and
`Sites/CartesianClosed.lean` obtains sheaf closedness by reflection. No extra
sheafification or exponential axioms are caller parameters. This scope includes
all small sites with sections in their common universe, and the finite diamond
with ordinary `Type` sections. The API does not claim the independent section
universe of the earlier image laws; larger section universes or essentially
small sites would need their own instance/comparison analysis.

`exponential A B` is Mathlib's internal hom sheaf. `productComparison` composes
`SheafRelation.productIso` with the inverse component of `tensorLeftIsoProd`.
The argument is the first coordinate: `curry` sends `A × X → B` to
`X → exponential A B`, and `evaluation` has domain
`A × exponential A B`. Public `beta` and `eta` prove both inverse laws for
arbitrary morphisms. `curry_parameter` handles precomposition by `Y → X`;
callers supply the map and the original morphism. `evaluation_curry_apply`
exposes beta at every stage without treating an exponential section as an
arbitrary function on that stage. `ClosedMap.lean` was inspected: its law
transport addresses a separate abstract map; this adapter needs only Mathlib's
adjunction laws and the two actual product comparisons.

[Examples/SheafFunctions.lean](../../Examples/SheafFunctions.lean) transposes
the existing natural Bool negation into a global element of the actual Bool
function sheaf. Evaluation at X returns true on false and false on true. Eta
recovers the named operation; parameter naturality proves that every Bool
parameter selects that same operation, and a stage calculation still uses
native Lean `Bool.not`. Thus ordinary calculations can mix sheaf morphisms and
native operations without a source-language wrapper.

The same file transposes the constant-false and constant-true maps `E → D`.
Their exponential sections at X differ: equality would restrict to W, where
evaluation at an E section gives the contradiction false = true. Yet the
external type `E(X) → D(X)` has a unique function, because E(X) is empty.
`no_pointwise_equivalence` rules out even a type equivalence at X. Native Lean
function spaces therefore cannot replace these sheaf function spaces.

The dependent-family construction below supplies the next comparison with
`Dependent.lean`; it does not follow from nondependent exponentials alone.

Dependent products, model maps, a smooth site and scalar, SDG axioms, higher
identities and cohesion remain separate unproved obligations. No completeness
or differential-cohesive-model claim follows from this fragment.

## Dependent families and split-family comparison

[#59](https://github.com/VladimirMarko/conaled/issues/59) constructs the next
fragment in [SquareKit/Family.lean](../../SquareKit/Family.lean). A family is an
actual morphism `p : A → Γ`; `Term p` is the subtype of sections with equation
`t ≫ p = identity`. For any category with pullbacks, `reindex p σ` is the
second projection of Mathlib's chosen pullback, and `comparison p σ` is its
first projection. `pair` uses the universal property. Its projection,
uniqueness and eta laws express context extension; `variableTerm` is the
diagonal section of p pulled back along itself. `termEquiv` identifies terms
over σ with maps a satisfying `a ≫ p = σ`.

`termSub` substitutes an actual section and proves both projection equations.
`identityIso` compares identity substitution with the original total space;
`compositionIso` reuses Mathlib's pullback pasting isomorphism. Their projection
laws show that they are comparisons over the base, and substituted terms agree
after transport. These are isomorphisms between chosen objects, not
identifications of those objects. Unit and associativity coherence between
multiple comparison isomorphisms, and a strict splitting, have not been proved.

[SquareKit/SheafFamily.lean](../../SquareKit/SheafFamily.lean) specializes this
interface to type-valued sheaves. Unlike the exponential adapter, this fragment
keeps indexing objects, arrows and section universes independent. Sheaf limits
supply the pullbacks; callers do not provide a family model or new gluing
proofs. `sectionsEquiv` compares each pullback's sections to compatible pairs.
`constantIso` identifies substitution of an explicit product projection with
the explicit product over the new base. `constant_pair` and `constant_termSub`
transport pairing and substitution through that comparison.

The optional
[regular-context adapter](../../SquareKit/Internal/SheafFamilies.lean) compares
existing extended contexts to those products. Its `substitutionIso` uses the
already proved contextual pullback square; its pairing law recovers every map
into an extended context through the new chosen pullback. The original regular
context API is unchanged.

[SquareKit/TypeFamily.lean](../../SquareKit/TypeFamily.lean) imports the existing
`Dependent.lean`. For arbitrary `A : Γ → Type u`, `display` is its Sigma
projection and `terms` is an equivalence between its dependent terms and
sections. The existing `lift` and `p` form a proved pullback, yielding
`pullbackIso` from `Ext Δ (tySub A σ)` to the chosen categorical pullback.
Projection, `tmSub`, `extend`, the generic `q`, and eta agree through the
comparison. This recovers the basic split-family operations without changing
that implementation or claiming that its Pi/Sigma certificates now interpret
in sheaves.

[Examples/SheafFamilies.lean](../../Examples/SheafFamilies.lean) uses the actual
constant-false morphism `1 → Bool`. At every stage its false fiber is inhabited
and its true fiber is empty. The public section comparison proves that pulling
back along the existing natural negation reverses these fibers. Pairing yields
a dependent value over the true point and a term of the further restricted
family. Public identity and composition transport laws apply to this term,
including substitution through Bool parameters and negation. There is no
section over all Bool values. These are ordinary morphism and native Bool
calculations, without source syntax.

The existing family `E → 1` supplies a different obstruction: its local image
is top, but `Term` is empty because a section would give an element of E(X).
The checked `locally_inhabited_without_term` states both facts together.
Existentially local witnesses cannot be used as dependent terms.

The next bounded slice should prove unit and associativity coherence for these
canonical substitution comparisons, including compatibility with substituted
terms and the split Type comparison. The present identity and binary
composition laws provide the inputs but do not prove that triple substitution
has path-independent transport. Dependent products still require a right
adjoint to reindexing; the ordinary exponential does not supply it. A source
dependent calculus, identity elimination, universes, a smooth site and scalar,
Kock–Lawvere, higher identities and cohesion remain unconstructed.
