## Inspected declarations and hypotheses

These are stronger current construction facts than the initial inventory recorded.

[`SquareKit/KunnethIndexed.lean`](../../../SquareKit/KunnethIndexed.lean) supplies the proposed ordinary shared layer already:

- `Indexed C` has base `C : Type u`, `[Category.{v} C]`, and `fibers : LocallyDiscrete Cᵒᵖ ⥤ᵖ Cat.{w,z}`. Thus fiber objects use `Type z`, fiber homs `Type w`, independently of the base universes. Monoidal/symmetric fibers, braided pullback functors, chosen pushforwards and ordinary adjunctions are supplied.
- `Coherent` asks that the selected identity/composition pullback comparisons are monoidal. Ordinary associator and unitor laws already belong to the supplied pseudofunctor.
- `Proper` separates admissibility from operations. It requires closure under identities, composition and actual pullbacks, PF for all coefficients on admissible maps, and BC when the appropriate leg is admissible. It does not add exceptional operations.
- `square` constructs the pullback-functor exchange from the selected compositors and commutativity. `pushComp` constructs the pushforward comparison by right-adjoint uniqueness. Neither is an arbitrary endpoint isomorphism.
- `kunnethIso laws h hf hg A B` derives the admissibility of the pulled-back leg and instantiates exactly the three invertibility requirements. `kunneth_counit` retains E after conversion to the chosen pushforward of the composite.

The existing author expression is therefore the ordinary Lean application `Indexed.kunnethIso laws h hf hg A B`. A routine author supplies the actual pullback witness, original admissibility witnesses and coefficient objects. A model builder provides the indexed operations and proper laws once. No new source parser is needed to use this construction.

[`SquareKit/KunnethTransport.lean`](../../../SquareKit/KunnethTransport.lean) constructs transport of a selected tensor transpose through an adjoint square. Its `TensorCompatible` law concerns the square and tensor structure for every pair of objects. `tensorCompatible_of_monoidal` derives it from native monoidality. `tensorTranspose_transport` asks for the two component evaluation compatibility equations `hx,hy`, then invokes [`AdjointSquareTransport.curry`](../../../SquareKit/AdjointSquareTransport.lean). `map_eq_tensorTranspose` connects this theorem to the independently constructed K. `tensorTranspose_transport_conjugate` proves T with specified input/output comparisons, and `inverse_transport` transports the chosen inverse.

This transport theorem is a reusable local theorem, not an implemented universal pseudonatural map builder for all indexed providers. The theorem does not assume T itself. `AdjointSquareTransport` also already derives `curry_paste`, `uncurry_paste`, and whole-transformation `mate_paste`, and recovers native dependent BC under the original assumptions. The initial report's suggested shared mate layer must account for these completed declarations.

## Actual model and witness coverage

[`SquareKit/KunnethPredicates.lean`](../../../SquareKit/KunnethPredicates.lean) implements fibers `Set (X × I)`, tensor by intersection, pullback by preimage, and pushforward by universal quantification along a function. Admissible maps are surjections. Their nonempty fibers prove PF; the actual pullback universal property proves BC. This is an abstract ordinary realization of the proper-law interface, not geometric properness or a motivic model.

For a color map `r:J→I`, `recolor r X` gives a fiber functor. `pullRoute`, `pushRoute`, `pushRoute_eq_mate`, and `route_counit` supply the actual comparisons and C. `map_transport` uses the shared tensor-transpose theorem. `output_composition` accounts for the chosen pushforward conversion and `indexed_transport` proves preservation of the indexed K map. Thinness discharges model-level structural comparison equations; the generic K/E/T proofs do not rely on thinness.

[`Examples/Kunneth.lean`](../../../Examples/Kunneth.lean) checks a noninjective surjection `Bool×Bool→Bool`, variable coefficients, the actual pullback, and distinct Unit/Bool-color models. A target coefficient outside the duplication route's image prevents conflating transport with coverage of every target object. `transported_map` and `transported_inverse` preserve the named maps and inverses. `empty_projection_not_iso` rejects the empty-to-singleton map's PF despite its ordinary adjunction.

The `SelectedMap` namespace is a non-thin control: its selected square maps `(a,b)` to `(a,(b,true))`; the false tag is missed, so the resulting map is not surjective. It retains both coefficients and a chosen tag, but it is not a full non-thin indexed proper model or a non-thin successful T route. Those stronger descriptions would overstate this evidence. Native slice projection and selected dependent-function checks also remain in the file.

**Adopt the existing direct/local K construction and its indexed derived layer for the ordinary checkpoint.** Their relationship already realizes the desired design: native direct clients retain their minimal inputs, while indexed builders install laws once and routine authors reuse the construction. This is capability evidence, not a measured author-effort ranking. The prepared author trial remains distinct work; the coordinator identified #604 as its open owner.

**Investigate H as a separate minimal extension**, keeping the higher witness experiment in the initial report as the presentation discriminator. The smallest new ordinary construction is a local `exceptionalHom` theorem over two closed symmetric monoidal fibers, `f* ⊣ f_*`, `f_! ⊣ f^!`, and a specified natural exceptional PF isomorphism. Derive the Hom-chain, naturality in the test object, the represented isomorphism and inverse laws. Do not demand a whole correspondence model, arbitrary BC, motivic geometry or exceptional coherence for unrelated maps merely to prove this fixed-map theorem. An indexed wrapper can then expose the admissible-map family without duplicating the proof. Missing exceptional adjunction or PF must reject the application precisely. A searched symbol inventory did not identify such a six-functor H module in the inspected source; this is an inventory result, not a proof that no equivalent theorem exists anywhere in Mathlib.

A higher candidate must recover those observations and additionally preserve a genuine dependent higher cell.
