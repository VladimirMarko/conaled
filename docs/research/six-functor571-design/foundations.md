# Foundations assessment for #571

Retain the direct ordinary interfaces and their derived coherence layer for the existing (K), (H), and (T) observation contract. Investigate a bounded higher witness representation alongside them. The current tree already constructs (K)/(E) and a generic transport theorem, and already implements ordinary span composition and its interpretation. Replacing those constructions would spend effort without addressing the remaining dimensional question. The strongest reason to retain them is that their proofs identify the selected maps, including counits, endpoint comparisons, payloads, and apex projections.

This recommendation does not identify ordinary equality with higher equality. Nor does it identify the all-apex-map bicategory in the repository with the correspondence category in Scholze's definition. Both distinctions affect recovery.

## Primary contracts

**Scholze:** Lecture I, pp. 6–8, treats derived sheaves on finite-dimensional locally compact Hausdorff spaces, with the locally closed Euclidean restriction, and derives proper Künneth from the specified BC/PF maps. Definitions 2.3–2.5, pp. 16–17, use spans with unrestricted left leg and right leg in `E`, a lax symmetric monoidal functor to `Cat∞`, and right adjoints to tensor, pullback, and shriek pushforward. The correspondence homs are groupoids, not categories of arbitrary apex maps. For ordinary `C`, the result is a `(2,1)`-category. Definition 2.3 assumes finite limits and closure of `E` under pullback/composition, containing isomorphisms; Lecture III explicitly also includes diagonals. Definition/Proposition 3.2 uses triangular simplices with cartesian small squares and downward-right arrows in `E`; its general horn-filling result is cited, not proved by writing the definition. The adjoint coherence assertion presupposes the coherent three-operation formalism. [October 2025 notes](https://people.mpim-bonn.mpg.de/scholze/SixFunctors.pdf).

**Cisinski–Déglise:** Example 1.4.3, p. 28, gives `SH` over noetherian schemes of finite dimension as a triangulated premotivic category; Example 2.4.48, p. 77, identifies it as motivic. Theorem 2.4.50, pp. 78–79, supplies exceptional adjunctions for separated finite-type maps, their 2-functor structure, a compatible proper comparison with ordinary pushforward, cartesian exceptional exchange, and PF/internal-Hom formulas. Orientation appears in the optional purity refinement, not as a premise of the PF/Hom formulas. Sections 1.1.15–16, pp. 8–9, construct exchange via adjunction and discuss pasting compatibility. Sections 1.1.31–32, p. 12, construct the ordinary pushforward projection map and discuss composition/mixed-exchange compatibility; some verification and formulation are left to the reader. These are ordinary categorical claims, not a supplied Lean higher enhancement. [arXiv v4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0912.2110v4).

## Contract audit: keep K, H, and T separate

The [contract report](https://github.com/VladimirMarko/conaled/blob/b202bc865dc3aaf135e117b647bef23bf3b269fa/docs/research/six-functor-reusable-argument.md) fixes the following distinctions.

* **K:** selected ordinary right adjoints, monoidal pullback, a genuine pullback square, properness of both maps, and invertibility of the canonical BC and the two PF components. Closure under pullback supplies properness of the intermediate projection. The conclusion is the particular composite, with its inverse. It requires neither exceptional operations nor internal Hom.
* **H:** closed symmetric monoidal fibers, `f* ⊣ f_*`, a separate `f_! ⊣ f^!`, and the exceptional PF natural isomorphism for the actual coefficients. The natural hom-set bijections produce the selected internal-Hom isomorphism by Yoneda. Exceptional PF is not a consequence of the existence of these two adjunctions alone. The nonproper open immersion in the contract is a test that cannot be discharged by proper comparison alone.
* **T:** tensor compatibility, actual pullback/ordinary-pushforward route comparisons, and their evaluation/counit compatibility identify the transported K map. Arbitrary endpoint isomorphisms do not suffice. Transport of this fragment establishes neither preservation of every internal Hom nor an exceptional exchange theorem.

The capability matrix must therefore retain separate rows for ordinary adjoints/tensor, exceptional adjoints, closedness, higher transformation coherence, motivic purity/localization, and full interpretation maps. No row is implied by the presence of six names.

## Dimensional and universe boundary

The direct indexed provider has four independent parameters: `C : Type u`, `Category.{v} C`, fiber objects in `Type z`, and fiber homs in `Type w`, via `LocallyDiscrete Cᵒᵖ ⥤ᵖ Cat.{w,z}`. `Cat.{w,z}` is bundled structure in `Type (max (z+1) (w+1))`; functors and natural transformations between these fixed fibers live at `Type (max z w)`. This follows from Mathlib's `Category : Type max z (w+1)` and `Bundled` universe declarations. The local K calculation permits four separately sized fibers (`u₁`–`u₄`, `v₁`–`v₄`). None of this resizes an arbitrarily large motivic category into the base universe.

The existing slice action instead takes `C : Type u`, `Category.{v} C`, and `HasPullbacks C`; `Over X` has objects at `Type (max u v)` and homs at `Type v`. Its target is exactly `Cat.{v,max u v}`. The current `SpanInterpretation` requires source/target bases to share `u,v`; `MixedBaseChange` already allows independently sized bases. Generalizing the former universe signature would be an interface extension, not evidence for a foundational obstruction.

For a bounded higher experiment, a precise size convention is small groupoids with objects `Type uG` and arrows `Type vG`; an isocomma apex has objects in `Type (max uG vG)` and arrows in `Type vG`. A general simplicial proposal can instead fix `κB,κF` and require base simplices `C_n : Type κB` and fiber simplices `D(X)_n : Type κF` for every natural `n`. Mapping-space simplices and each finite triangular correspondence diagram remain within the corresponding chosen bound; the collection of such small structures requires the successor universe. This convention is a proposed uniform size discipline, not an implemented `Cat∞` API or a size theorem for `SH`.

| Observation | Direct ordinary / derived layer | Higher presentation requirement |
|---|---|---|
| Objects and coefficient fibers | Actual objects and dependent payloads; no quotient required. | Retain actual objects and dependent coefficient transport. |
| Arrows and spans | Actual morphisms, actual chosen apex and both legs. | Retain maps and correspondence representatives, not only connected components. |
| Transformations | Natural transformations and specified natural isomorphisms are data. Arbitrary apex maps exist in local `SpanBicat`. | Specify whether this is `(∞,1)` or `(∞,2)` structure. The source `Corr` only has invertible higher cells between correspondences. |
| Coherence between transformations | Equality in `Prop`; associator/unitor components remain data, but proofs of their equations are proof-irrelevant. | Observable paths between transformations must be encoded as data, with their endpoints and composition. |
| Next dimension and above | No observable distinction between equality proofs. | Specify the next cells and laws or an unbounded simplicial representation. A finite truncation must disclose the truncation. |

Lean's proof irrelevance does not force every representation to discard paths: a groupoid arrow or a simplex can be a `Type`-valued datum while equations asserting its faces are propositions. Conversely, changing a field named `coherence` from one proposition to another proposition cannot recover lost higher paths. A nerve of an ordinary category is a useful special case, but does not create missing higher homotopies.

## Three candidates

**Direct ordinary operations.** Retain for local K/H proofs and native callers. Operations, transformation directions, and exact coefficient assumptions stay visible. Identity/composition equations are ordinary equations; in the indexed entry point the pseudofunctor supplies their organization. The advantage is the checked small-contract K theorem and its weaker oplax input. The cost is that a route builder must assemble the component compatibility hypotheses. A separate theorem for H can follow the same design without enlarging native dependent clients. This presentation fully expresses the chosen ordinary contract and does not express untruncated higher witness observations.

**Small derived coherence layer.** Adopt the layer already present and investigate additions only where a second consumer needs them. `AdjointSquareTransport` derives curry transport and pasting; `AdjointComparison` conjugates the same mate when chosen adjoints change; `KunnethTransport` uses those results rather than installing another mates engine. Monoidality of an indexed compositor is an extra compatibility law on existing operations and is correctly separated from ordinary pseudofunctor coherence. Ordinary BC invertibility and PF are model theorems, not derived from a commuting square. A reusable route builder could discharge repeated K transport equations from monoidal pseudonaturality and canonical mates; that is a possible future convenience, not a missing proof of the current local theorem. Do not add fields asserting K, T, or all pasting equations when they follow from primitive laws.

**Higher correspondence encoding.** Investigate for source-level higher observations, defer adoption as the common implementation interface. For a proper fragment, choose right legs in `P`, left legs unrestricted, with identities/composition/pullback closure. For the full proposed formalism, choose right legs in `E` and include the specified diagonal condition; tensor also needs finite-product structure. The existing all-leg slice action needs only pullbacks and must retain that weaker assumption. A higher interface needs a construction of the correspondence domain, its monoidal structure, the interpretation, its transformations, and the mapping-space adjunction equivalences. An abbreviation containing `Corr` or `Cat∞` supplies none of these proofs. Once supplied, higher mate coherence should be derived from adjunction structure; it should not become an unrelated infinite list of user axioms.

The representation comparison is not a total order. The local bicategory has noninvertible apex maps, whereas the specified higher source has only equivalences in correspondence hom-groupoids. Restriction to invertible apex maps preserves a common portion. It cannot promise recovery of `collapse : duplicate ⟶ unduplicated`. Retaining that operation requires a separate ordinary API or an explicitly richer higher domain. Nor can taking the homotopy category of the higher source recover all selected paths. Any claimed roundtrip must name its subdomain and its observation dimension.

## Existing composition and interpretation evidence

[SpanAction.lean](https://github.com/VladimirMarko/conaled/blob/b202bc865dc3aaf135e117b647bef23bf3b269fa/SquareKit/SpanAction.lean) sends `X ←a W →b Y` to `Over.pullback a ⋙ Over.map b`. Both leg classes are `⊤`; no properness, finite-limit preservation, monoidal structure, or exceptional functor is required. `actionMap` acts on arbitrary apex maps. `compositionIso` and its inverse reassociate actual chosen pullbacks. `composition_value`, `composition_first`, and `composition_second` retain every coordinate. `compositionIso_eq_beckChevalley` identifies the entire isomorphism with the existing BC construction. `pseudofunctor` proves both whiskerings, associator compatibility, and both units by projection extensionality. These are derived laws, not caller fields awaiting proof.

The [three-span client](https://github.com/VladimirMarko/conaled/blob/b202bc865dc3aaf135e117b647bef23bf3b269fa/Examples/SpanAction.lean) starts from payload `⟨3,last : Fin 4⟩`, chooses apex `(3,true)`, matches doubled index `6` via apex `3`, then chooses `(4,1)` and forgets the final base to `PUnit`. Its four output observations recover the payload and each of the three apex coordinates. The associator equation is evaluated on this actual input, and both unit equations are specialized. The final base alone cannot distinguish these selected values. The collapse control identifies Boolean branches while preserving payload; it witnesses an intentional loss by a noninvertible map, not a failure of the compositor.

[SpanInterpretation.lean](https://github.com/VladimirMarko/conaled/blob/b202bc865dc3aaf135e117b647bef23bf3b269fa/SquareKit/SpanInterpretation.lean) uses a pullback-preserving `F`, its actual chosen pullback comparison, and `Over.post F`. `transformation` is a `StrongTrans`; its components need not be equivalences. The whole composition equation includes the composite-apex comparison. `beckChevalley_transport` then identifies preservation of the specified BC isomorphism. Its [client](https://github.com/VladimirMarko/conaled/blob/b202bc865dc3aaf135e117b647bef23bf3b269fa/Examples/SpanInterpretation.lean) composes `Over base → Over Bool → Type`, retaining the chosen payload and apex through both comparisons. The proof that `Over.forget Bool` fails finite-limit preservation prevents silently strengthening the interface. Identity/composite equations in the interpretation variable are present at the span/component level; #633 owns their further packaging as pseudofunctor comparisons and invertible modifications.

Native recovery must remain under the original assumptions. `native_recovery`, `native_twice_recovery`, and `native_tagged_recovery` recover whole `Selected` values, including the alternative adjoint representation, for arbitrary type families and substitutions. [DependentBaseChange](https://github.com/VladimirMarko/conaled/blob/b202bc865dc3aaf135e117b647bef23bf3b269fa/SquareKit/DependentBaseChange.lean) takes pullbacks and actual supplied adjunctions; its default pulled-back adjoint is constructed by [ExponentiablePullback](https://github.com/VladimirMarko/conaled/blob/b202bc865dc3aaf135e117b647bef23bf3b269fa/SquareKit/ExponentiablePullback.lean). Invertibility of its dependent mate follows by identification with conjugation of a proven left comparison, not by the false claim that all mates preserve isomorphisms. Evaluation, transpose, and curry laws retain selected arrows. [MixedBaseChange](https://github.com/VladimirMarko/conaled/blob/b202bc865dc3aaf135e117b647bef23bf3b269fa/SquareKit/MixedBaseChange.lean) constructs comparison/pasting without any adjoint, and requires preservation only for invertibility. [GeometricMorphism](https://github.com/VladimirMarko/conaled/blob/b202bc865dc3aaf135e117b647bef23bf3b269fa/SquareKit/Internal/GeometricMorphism.lean) additionally requires finite-limit preservation and keeps inverse-image transformations and specified lift comparisons. Its stronger hypothesis belongs to that interface, not to every span client.

## Proposed author forms and rejection obligations

These are proposed author-facing expressions, not a parser claim:

```text
relativeKunneth D laws square proper_f proper_g A B
transport selectedK along route using its monoidal adjoint comparisons
exceptionalHom D exceptional_f A B
```

The first already has the Lean endpoint `Indexed.kunnethIso laws square proper_f proper_g A B`. The elaborator must infer the four fibers, derive properness of the pullback leg, use the canonical mate/PF terms, and choose `pushComp` from adjoint uniqueness. It must reject the empty-map predicate control when PF invertibility is requested. A cartesian witness supplies the square, not that law.

Transport elaboration must preserve input/output isomorphisms, their inverses, the chosen composite adjoint, and the route's second component equation involving the square. Native monoidality may discharge `TensorCompatible`; arbitrary natural isomorphisms cannot. For a paper counterexample, take a constant ordinary model with fiber rational vector spaces, all pullbacks/pushforwards identity, and an identity route. Replacing its pushforward comparison at the identity by minus identity makes the two input signs cancel but leaves a negative output sign in T. The endpoint isomorphisms exist, but the purported route violates counit/unit compatibility. This separates the missing requirement from endpoint typing. It is a proposed mathematical control, not a checked example here.

For H, require closed tensor adjunctions in both fibers, ordinary and exceptional adjunctions for this `f`, and the specified exceptional PF for every variable needed by the Yoneda calculation. Construct the natural bijection in test object `T`, including symmetry and variance in `A,B`, then obtain the isomorphism. If only an ordinary `Indexed` provider is supplied, reject `push!`/`pull!` at the missing exceptional structure. If only proper `f_! ≅ f_*` is available, reject the nonproper open-immersion example. If transporting H, require the additional compatible exceptional and closed comparisons that the route actually needs. Strong monoidality alone is insufficient: extension of scalars from abelian groups to rational vector spaces sends `Hom_Z(Q,Z)=0` to zero, while the internal Hom of the images is `Hom_Q(Q,Q)=Q`. This is another paper control, not an executed model.

A higher author form may share the calculation syntax only after its judgment says whether a comparison is an ordinary isomorphism or a selected higher equivalence and whether T is an equality or a specified path. Missing horn/coherence or mapping-space adjunction data must be a model-construction error; it must not be filled by proof irrelevance or inferred from operation names.

## Smallest discriminating next implementation

Do not rebuild #569/#632, and do not use #633's still-owned interpretation packaging as an implicit prerequisite. The smallest higher experiment should test one information boundary before a full six-functor interface:

1. Use small groupoids and a single isocomma pullback. Its objects are `(x,y,α : F x ≅ G y)` and its arrows retain the equation relating `α` to the two component maps. For the two maps `* → B(C₂) ← *`, prove that the isocomma has two distinguishable objects and only identity arrows. The distinction is the chosen comparison `α`. A strict object-equality pullback has one object; it cannot recover both selected comparisons.
2. Compose three selected correspondences at this bounded level, retain both comparison arrows, and prove the associator and unit functors' projection formulas. Include a map of these diagrams that carries each selected comparison arrow to its specified image. State that this experiment is groupoid-level, not a construction of the full `Corr(C,E) → Cat∞` formalism.
3. On discrete groupoids, prove recovery of the ordinary chosen pullback and both projections up to the explicit equivalence. Reuse the existing three-span/native clients through that recovery contract. Do not require terminal preservation or exceptional operations. Preserve the ordinary noninvertible-apex-map client outside any restriction to the correspondence core.
4. Include a transformation-level control: the identity functor of `B(C₂)` has distinct natural automorphisms given by the two central group elements. Store those transformations as data and show their distinction survives the chosen interpretation. Quotienting to an isomorphism class of functors must fail the corresponding reconstruction claim. This still tests only a bounded dimension; higher paths between transformations remain unimplemented.
