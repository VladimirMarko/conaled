Retain the existing ordinary Künneth and transport APIs, use their derived coherence rather than adding parallel axioms, and investigate a bounded groupoid correspondence adapter before choosing a full higher encoding. The strongest evidence for the present design is working mathematics at the required ordinary observation level. `SquareKit/Kunneth.lean` independently constructs the PF–BC–PF composite and proves `counit_characterization`. `KunnethIndexed.lean` supplies its indexed form, using the pseudofunctor's actual comparison maps and adjunction-induced `pushComp`. `KunnethTransport.lean` proves transport of the specified composite through its transpose, including conjugation and inverse transport. These implement the K/T checkpoint; a recommendation to rebuild them from the earlier #557 report would be stale. `KunnethPredicates.lean` and `Examples/Kunneth.lean` provide a concrete interpretation route and a separate nonthin selected-map control. They do not construct motivic categories or a higher six-operation formalism.

The primary sources distinguish those obligations. Scholze's Definitions 2.3–2.5 describe correspondence morphisms using the groupoid of spans and a lax symmetric monoidal functor to `Cat∞`; the full six-functor condition adds the specified existence of right adjoints. Definition/Proposition 3.2 uses simplices of cartesian diagrams, with horn filling as a mathematical assertion, not a benefit obtained by writing span notation. For an ordinary base, this correspondence construction retains invertible apex comparisons, whereas the repository's ordinary span bicategory also accepts noninvertible apex maps. Lecture I supplies the derived-sheaf Künneth example. [Scholze, October 2025, printed pp. 6–8, 16–19](https://people.mpim-bonn.mpg.de/scholze/SixFunctors.pdf).

Cisinski–Déglise construct exchange and projection maps from adjunctions and record their compatibilities in §§1.1.15–16 and 1.1.31–32. Their Theorem 2.4.50 applies to a motivic triangulated category and separated finite-type maps: it supplies exceptional adjunctions, the proper comparison, exceptional base change, and the projection/internal-Hom formulas. Examples 1.4.3 and 2.4.48 identify `SH` as an instance. This is applicability evidence for the paper contracts, not a Lean construction of that model. [Cisinski–Déglise, arXiv v4, printed pp. 8–12, 28, 77–79](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0912.2110v4).

The size and equality contracts should remain explicit. The ordinary indexed interface has base `C : Type u`, base arrows in `Type v`, fiber objects in `Type z`, and fiber arrows in `Type w`; it targets `Cat.{w,z}`. The direct four-category K theorem permits the four object and hom universes independently. `SpanAction` has base `Type u`/`Category.{v}`, apex objects in the same base, and targets `Cat.{v,max u v}` because a slice object includes a domain and an arrow. The inspected `SpanInterpretation` currently takes both bases at the same `u,v`; the lower-level mixed-base-change interface permits independently sized categories. A new adapter should expose any universe alignment rather than silently promise the latter generality through the former interface.

For K, the supplied proper class contains identities and is closed under composition and pullback; BC and PF concern the canonical maps, with PF for every coefficient object. The span recovery control has **all** legs admissible and requires only pullbacks. Its action is `Over.pullback l ⋙ Over.map r`, a pullback followed by dependent sum; `Over.map` is not the right-adjoint pushforward appearing in K. Reusing its coherence does not identify those operations. A proposed higher six-operation adapter must state its own exceptional right-leg class, its closure conditions (including the diagonal condition in the frozen higher contract), its proper subclass, and the proper comparison. None of these is supplied by the unconstrained span control.

The full higher construction would also need monoidal structure, adjunctions and coherent transformations between interpretations. Its existence does not follow from a bounded diagram adapter. Conversely, proof-irrelevant *laws* do not erase the data that they constrain. Mathlib's `Quasicategory` class is propositional horn-existence certification over a simplicial set whose simplices remain data. A client observing a selected filler must retain that filler explicitly. `Quasicategory/StrictBicategory.lean` applies `hoFunctor` to simplicial internal homs; that is useful formal category theory, but it is not a theorem that every higher witness survives. It must not be substituted for the full simplicial hom data when the contract observes those witnesses.

The requested ordinary composition control is already concrete. In `Examples/SpanAction.lean`, the three spans duplicate `(n,b)`, match a doubled index, then forget the final base. The selected chain retains payload `⟨3,3⟩` and apex coordinates `(3,true)`, `3`, `(4,1)`, although its final base is `PUnit`. The example evaluates `map₂_associator` on the actual input and checks both unit laws. `compositionIso_eq_beckChevalley` identifies the entire compositor with the independently assembled base-change route. The noninvertible `collapse` map loses the Boolean while retaining the payload; the branches were proved distinct beforehand. An odd unmatched index is rejected. These are stronger controls than endpoint existence.

`SpanInterpretation.transformation` uses a pullback-preserving base functor, the actual `Over.post` components, and `actionIso`; its composition and apex-map equations are proved. `composition_transport` and `beckChevalley_transport` preserve the selected comparison. `Examples/SpanInterpretation.lean` uses actual forgetful interpretations, a selected dependent payload and apex, and successive comparisons. The collapse-to-`Nonempty` example fails pullback preservation. The `Over Bool` forgetful control preserves pullbacks but fails finite-limit preservation, so strengthening the span adapter to left exactness would exclude an existing intended client. Packaging comparisons in the interpretation variable remains #633's separate scope.

Native recovery also has an exact existing obligation to reuse: `native_recovery`, `native_twice_recovery` and `native_tagged_recovery` in `Examples/SpanAction.lean` recover the whole selected output under arbitrary type families and substitutions, including an alternative chosen sum adjunction. `DependentBaseChange`, `AdjointComparison` and `MixedBaseChange` explain the comparisons without imposing a geometric morphism or global locally cartesian closure. `Examples/IndexedWitnessAdjunctions.lean` supplies nonconstant finite families and rules out reconstruction from mere existence. A higher adapter should specialize back to these equations under these assumptions; requiring a motivic model to obtain them would be a regression.

For authors, the following are **proposed expressions**, not a parser or new checked declarations:

```text
K := kunnethIso proper square A B
H := exceptionalHomIso exceptional f A B
T := transportSpecifiedK route K
```

K elaboration must determine the cartesian square, the proper witnesses, all coefficients, the actual PF/BC maps, and the induced right-adjoint composition. Existing declarations already implement this obligation. T must identify the transported counits and tensor comparison with those used by the target K; arbitrary endpoint isomorphisms are insufficient. `tensorTranspose_transport` and `tensorCompatible_of_monoidal` provide the current derived route, rather than a new independent coherence axiom. The existing predicate route is a useful checked instance, while the nonthin selected-map example prevents thinness alone from establishing faithfulness to the chosen map. #604 remains the owner of the comparative author trial; this assessment measures no author cost.

H elaboration additionally needs closed fibers, `f* ⊣ f_*`, `f_! ⊣ f^!`, and exceptional PF for the required objects. For a fixed map, its paper derivation is the natural chain

```text
Hom(C, [f_! A,B]) ≅ Hom(C ⊗ f_! A,B)
  ≅ Hom(f_!(f* C ⊗ A),B) ≅ Hom(f* C ⊗ A,f^! B)
  ≅ Hom(f* C,[A,f^! B]) ≅ Hom(C,f_*[A,f^! B]).
```

Yoneda gives the specified internal-Hom isomorphism. This proposal is not checked here. A builder with only proper K data must fail to elaborate H for a general nonproper map; replacing `f_!` by `f_*` would change the claim. An interpretation supplied with unrelated pushforward isomorphisms but no canonical-mate/counit compatibility must likewise fail the T constructor. H does not justify adding the entire exceptional formalism to ordinary K callers. #572/#573 retain the motivic/exceptional implementation obligations.

The smallest discriminating next implementation is a **groupoid boundary experiment**, not a new K proof or an alleged full `Corr∞`:

1. Reuse `Examples.SecondIdentity.Label`, `Bit` and their checked group isomorphism. Form the two point functors `1 → B(Label)` and their comma groupoid. Its objects carry an actual boundary arrow. For the two-element group there should be two distinguishable boundary labels. Construct these data and observations in Lean; do not represent the boundary by equality of the sole base object. `QuotientPointedArcs.Pullback`, its groupoid instance and boundary-retaining `comparisonIso` are a checked specialized pattern to inspect, not an already generic groupoid-span compositor.
2. Compose the spans `1 ← 1 → B(Label)` and `B(Label) ← 1 → 1`, then a span `1 ← Discrete Bool → 1`. Build both iterated comma presentations and the reassociation equivalence, preserving the boundary label and Boolean. Check the selected associativity and unit comparison equations. A generic reusable composition API would additionally need its full naturality and pentagon laws; the bounded experiment must not claim those from one selected chain.
3. Transport the experiment along the existing `Bit ≃* Label` representation change. Prove preservation of the actual boundary observation and both composite projections. For discrete groupoids, relate this construction to the existing ordinary pullback and three-span/native recovery controls, rather than replace them.
4. Check the negative: discarding the comma boundary gives only the strict object-level pullback of the two point maps and cannot recover both labels. This is a proposed checked counterexample, presently a paper calculation. In parallel reuse the already checked, genuinely second-dimensional control `SecondIdentity.cell_distinct`, `routes_agree`, `route_roundtrip`, `no_groupoid_observation` and `no_native_identity_observation`. It distinguishes 2-cell labels and shows that a locally discrete or host-equality replacement cannot recover their observations. The comma boundary and this 2-cell control test different dimensions; passing the first does not establish the second.

For this finite pilot all objects/arrows can live in `Type 0`; its reusable groupoid version should expose object universe `u` and arrow universe `v`, with comma objects in `Type (max u v)` and comma arrows in `Type v`. This avoids a hidden smallness assumption. The pilot would establish a witness-preserving finite comparison, not horn filling in every dimension or a six-functor model. Only after its result should a follow-up choose a simplicial correspondence construction and prove its recovery theorem and higher interpretation laws.

Therefore **reject** quotienting observable comparisons, pretending arbitrary endpoint isomorphisms prove T, or using ordinary proper data as exceptional structure. **Defer** a general higher six-functor package and foundation migration. **Investigate** the bounded boundary adapter and any independently demonstrated elaboration bottleneck. **Adopt** the existing ordinary and derived coherence APIs for their established contracts. No timing or productivity ranking follows from these choices.
