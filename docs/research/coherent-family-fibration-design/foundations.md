## Why the recovery claim follows

`SquareKit/Internal/CoherentFamilyClassification.lean:18` defines `Coherent E V A` by preservation of every arrow-category square whose right component is invertible. Its full subcategory retains every natural transformation. For an arrow `f : x ⟶ y`, `evaluationArrow E f` is the square from `f` to `id_y` with components `(f, id_y)`. Its right component is invertible, so coherence supplies precisely the invertibility needed for recovery. No invertibility of `f`, of all squares, or of arbitrary diagram morphisms is assumed.

The existing `recovery` at line 49 constructs `A ≅ rightFunc ⋙ identityArrow ⋙ A` from these squares. Naturality in arrow squares follows from the square equation and functor composition. `recoveryIso` at line 66 then proves naturality in arbitrary natural transformations. Consequently the existing equivalence at line 74 includes all noninvertible maps. The proposed total comparison extends this established equivalence; it does not need a new equivalence axiom on diagrams.

The full restriction condition is also justified by the intended recovered essential image. Any diagram naturally isomorphic to `rightFunc ⋙ B` sends every square with invertible right component to an isomorphism: `B` preserves that isomorphism, and naturality conjugates its image by the comparison components. Conversely the existing recovery establishes membership in that essential image. A potentially smaller primitive presentation would require invertibility only of the evaluation squares and derive the present condition, but changing the current interface offers no necessary benefit for this bridge.

The negative control is adequate. `Examples/CoherentFamilyClassification.lean:80–96` defines `badData` using the left endpoint and proves that its value at the selected arrow cannot even be isomorphic to its identity-target value. This excludes recovery after removing the condition, not just failure of one proposed proof. The same file retains a twisted Boolean presentation whose recovery swaps values and a collapse map proved noninvertible. These controls prevent accidental replacement of the full category by a core or replacement of nonidentity recovery by definitional equality.

## From modifications to an equivalence over the base

`SquareKit/Internal/RepresentableCoherentRecovery.lean:19–36` supplies the two strict context-indexed functors. Its `decoder` and `coder` at lines 40 and 82 are StrongTrans, with the decoder's invertible naturality and identity/composition equations already proved. `classificationUnit` and `classificationCounit` at lines 119 and 140 are isomorphisms of strong transformations, hence invertible modifications. The unit modification uses the existing `recovery_reindex` theorem, so recovery coherence across nonidentity substitutions is already available.

Pinned Mathlib `Mathlib/CategoryTheory/Bicategory/Grothendieck.lean:334` defines the total map of a StrongTrans. A total arrow `(σ, h)` maps to `(σ, α(h) ≫ α.naturality(σ))`. Its identity and composition obligations are proved there from StrongTrans coherence. `map_comp_forget` proves strict preservation of the base, and `mapIdIso`/`mapCompIso` at lines 373/384 supply the comparison isomorphisms required to assemble the inverse pair. Reproving total-map laws in the coherent-family module would duplicate this infrastructure.

The proposed narrow missing adapter is mathematically sufficient. For a modification `m : α ⟶ β`, define its total component at `(S, a)` as the inclusion into the total category of the fibre map `m_S(a)`. In the general pseudo case, this is `(id_S, m_S(a) ≫ G.mapId(S).inv(β_S(a)))`, not merely `(id_S, m_S(a))`. Mathlib's fibre inclusion `CoGrothendieck.ι` already contains this identity comparison. Total naturality follows from component naturality of `m_S` and the modification equation in `Bicategory/Modification/Pseudo.lean:51–58`. No additional mathematical compatibility law is missing.

Prove identity and vertical-composition preservation for that adapter, or build the resulting functor on the category of strong transformations. Invertible modifications then map to natural isomorphisms without separate inverse-law assumptions. Compose the lifted classification unit/counit with `mapIdIso` and `mapCompIso` in their required directions. The total unit and counit must have identity base components. Merely proving both functors commute with projection and obtaining an arbitrary equivalence would leave this part of “equivalence over the base” undocumented.

There is a small implementation choice around the triangle identity. The existing fibre equivalence proves it at identity views. Either transfer this calculation through the total adapter and its coherence, or use Mathlib's equivalence construction from functors and inverse isomorphisms if it adjusts one inverse isomorphism. In the latter route, document the adjustment and prove it still lies over identity. A chosen triangle field should not be added to the classification interface: the needed ordinary equivalence follows from the supplied inverse natural isomorphisms.

## Cartesian arrows and fibre recovery

`Mathlib/CategoryTheory/FiberedCategory/Grothendieck.lean:42` defines the canonical lift of `σ : R ⟶ S` into `(S, a)` as `(σ, id)` with source `(R, F(σ)(a))`. The same module proves its strong cartesian property, supplies `IsFibered` at line 78, and supplies `HasFibers` at line 138. Its `ι` at line 88 and `HasFibers.inducedFunctor` give the equivalence between the selected category `F(S)` and the categorical fibre. Import and expose these results; do not ask each coherent family to supply them.

For any StrongTrans α, its total map sends the canonical lift to `(σ, α.naturality(σ).app a)`, after the functor identity simplification. Its source differs from the canonical target-system source by the invertible vertical component of that naturality isomorphism. Thus the image factors as this vertical isomorphism followed by the target canonical lift. Mathlib `FiberedCategory/Cartesian.lean` provides composition and isomorphism cartesian results, so the preservation theorem follows for the selected lifts directly from StrongTrans.

The general preservation statement follows as well. Any cartesian arrow over σ is isomorphic over identity to the canonical lift with the same target; Mathlib's `IsCartesian.domainUniqueUpToIso` records this uniqueness. A functor preserves the vertical isomorphism, and the canonical-image argument handles the lift. In an `IsFibered` category the corresponding strong cartesian property is available. No equivalence assumption is needed for this preservation result, only StrongTrans's invertible naturality. This makes a generic helper appropriate if it stays small.

Identity and composition of the lift comparison are already consequences of StrongTrans's unit/composition laws. Test the comparison on ordered nonidentity substitutions, including the decoder's nontrivial components. Equality of separately selected `HasFibers.mkPullback` objects is neither supplied nor needed: those choices can differ from the explicit CoGrothendieck canonical lift by vertical isomorphism.

## Established scope defect: the brief omits a universe level

The brief says the context base has objects in `Type (max v (u+1))` for independently quantified `C : Type v` and `[Category.{w} C]`. The actual definition is `Context.{u} C := Cᵒᵖ ⥤ Type (max u w)` in `RepresentableClassification.lean:11`. Mathlib's `Functor` structure explicitly lives in `Type (max v₁ v₂ u₁ u₂)` (`CategoryTheory/Functor/Basic.lean:44–45`). Therefore the context object's universe is `max v (u+1) (w+1)`. Dropping `w+1` requires an additional level inequality or specialization absent from the contract.

Smallest correction: retain independent levels and replace that sentence with the actual base universe. Do not specialize `w` to make the shorter sentence true. The object/hom universe parameters of `Cat` and the two total categories should still be checked with elaborated signatures before acceptance. From the definitions, the expected selected fibre object level is `max u v w a b` and the expected fibre hom level is `max u v w b`; these latter estimates are paper calculations, not elaborated checks. A discriminating specialization must let `w` exceed both `u` and `v`, since an all-small example cannot detect the omission.

## Acceptance consequences

The mathematical route is supported by the existing source. The remaining claims are proof and adapter work, not a need for stronger assumptions. Acceptance should exhibit the actual total functors, unit/counit naturality on arrows with nonidentity base and noninvertible vertical part, identity base components of recovery, canonical cartesian comparison and its composition, and the selected HasFibers recovery. Reuse the existing twisted and failure controls under their original hypotheses. Keep the universe correction visible in the design record and verify its general specialization in Lean.

This bridge concerns ordinary categories over `Context C`. The evidence provides no dependent 2-cell transport, higher cubical coherence, small classifier construction, or recovery for arbitrary context-indexed pseudofunctors. The brief correctly leaves those claims outside #705.
