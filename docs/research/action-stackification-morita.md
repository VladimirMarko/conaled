# Action presentations, stackification and atlas refinement

The consumer selected for [#678](https://github.com/VladimirMarko/conaled/issues/678)
and [#679](https://github.com/VladimirMarko/conaled/issues/679) is the labelled
chart presentation of a sheaf group action and its refinement by a locally
surjective atlas. The quotient already exists as a stack of torsors with
equivariant target maps. The additional requirement is to recover that stack
from its local chart presentation and to justify replacing the chart atlas.

## Contract

For a site `(C, J)`, a sheaf group `G`, and an action on a sheaf `M`, the
parameter category is `Sheaf J (Type (max u v))`. Its topology is
`SheafCover.topology`, generated by locally surjective maps. The action
presentation at `X` has objects `m : X ⟶ M`. An arrow from `m` to `n` is a
label `g : X ⟶ groupSheaf G` satisfying `a.onMap g n = m`. Labels remain
part of the arrow even when they act trivially. Restriction uses composition
of sheaf maps.

The output is the existing `SheafActionQuotient.Stack.pseudofunctor a`.
Its objects are torsors with equivariant target maps, and its arrows preserve
both structures. `SheafActionPresentation.transformation` sends a chart to
the regular torsor and sends its label to the corresponding equivariant
arrow. `quotientHomEquiv` recovers the label from every such arrow, proving
full faithfulness without an effectiveness assumption on the action.

Local generation uses the total space of each torsor, which is a cover of
its parameter. The tautological section trivializes the pulled-back torsor
and constructs its local chart and equivariant target map. No global
trivialization is assumed. The full subpresentation of globally trivializable
quotient objects is an intermediate presentation; `trivialEquivalence` and
`trivialTransformation` compare it with the independent labelled charts.

The generic descent arguments use Mathlib pseudofunctors into `Cat`, strong
transformations, modifications and descent categories. The category of
parameters has pullbacks. The local-generation hypothesis uses singleton
covers and includes an isomorphism to a source image. Categories and morphisms
retain explicit universes; the argument does not require essentially small
fibers or replace morphisms by their isomorphism classes.

## Universal mapping property

`StackificationUniversal.universalEquivalence` takes a strong transformation
`η : F ⟶ B` whose components are fully faithful and whose images locally
generate `B`. For every target stack `T` in the same `Cat` universe, it gives
`(B ⟶ T) ≌ (F ⟶ T)`. Its forward functor is actual precomposition with `η`;
the arrows in these categories are modifications. The extension is constructed
by lifting source cocycles and realizing their images using target descent.
Common cover refinements give independence, naturality and composition.
`extensionRecovery` recovers the original transformation on `F`.

Only the target `T` must be a stack for this mapping theorem. The selected
output `B` is independently known to be the torsor quotient stack.
`SheafActionPresentation.universalEquivalence` therefore identifies that
quotient as the stackification of the literal labelled charts.
`SheafActionRefinement.refinementUniversalEquivalence` applies the same
construction directly to the atlas map, while `quotientUniversalEquivalence`
identifies the refined presentation's completion with the same quotient.
Neither presentation is assumed to be a stack or fiberwise equivalent to it.

## What the controls establish

`Examples.ActionStackification` uses the existing nontrivial quarter-turn
clutching torsor. Its total-space cover carries compatible local chart data.
A global chart realizing those data would give a global section of the
torsor, contradicting the existing no-section theorem. Arrow descent already
holds in the input prestack; effective object descent is what is missing.
The globally trivializable subpresentation exhibits the same obstruction.
The already-stack control applies the local-generation equivalence criterion
to the existing quotient stack itself.

`Examples.ActionMorita` refines the disk action by principal opens excluding
first-coordinate levels `1/2` and `-1/2`. A second atlas uses `1/3` and
`-1/3`; their product functions give a common refinement. The origin has two
distinct atlas lifts, and every ordered pair of those lifts retains all four
quarter-turn labels. An ineffective action supplies a separate control that
label recovery does not depend on effectivity. The comparison with a discrete
orbit observation fails faithfulness, so an orbit-set theorem would not
establish the required presentation comparison.

The refinement construction takes a locally surjective map `p : U ⟶ M`.
Its objects are maps into `U`; its arrows are the original action labels
between their composites with `p`. `toPresentation` preserves those labels.
Local lifts follow from the pullback of `p`, giving full faithfulness and
local essential surjectivity. A global lift into the atlas is not assumed.
Identity and composite atlas refinements and parameter restriction preserve
the actual object and arrow maps.

## Existing clients and scope

The labelled presentation recovers the original smooth action-arrow API
through `smoothArrowEquiv`. The site-change comparison in
`SheafActionPresentationRecovery` gives a functor on labelled charts and
proves its compatibility with both the quotient functor and parameter
restriction under the original site-comparison assumptions. The direct
torsor and quotient-stack APIs remain the underlying output construction.

The local-branch theorem from [#717](proper-local-branches.md) supplies
geometric input when a presentation has a proper anchor and local source
inverses. Those hypotheses are separate from the categorical descent
argument here. The selected example does not establish a general comparison
for arbitrary proper étale or Lie groupoids, a bibundle calculus, higher
stackification, geometric representability, microlinearity or internal
`DPProper`. The broader bridge remains owned by
[#32](https://github.com/VladimirMarko/conaled/issues/32); presentation work is
coordinated through [#666](https://github.com/VladimirMarko/conaled/issues/666)
and [#647](https://github.com/VladimirMarko/conaled/issues/647).

Adopt the labelled presentation when the input consists of chart maps and
group labels, and use the refinement comparison when changing an atlas.
Keep the direct torsor quotient API for globally supplied torsors and target
maps. Both routes share the quotient construction and the generic descent
proof. Callers establish full faithfulness and local generation once; they do
not supply a universal property or prove extension coherence again. The
concrete atlas comparison and modification equivalences exercise these public
results. This establishes reuse, without a claim of general author productivity
or a reason to migrate existing direct clients.

The selected construction follows the mathematical distinction between
sheafifying arrows and adjoining effective objects in
[Stacks Project, Lemma 8.8.1](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/02ZN).
The proved universal property is an equivalence of categories of
morphisms into each target stack, including transformations between them,
as in [Lemma 8.8.3](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/04W9).
[Proposition 95.15.3](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/04WM) identifies an
action-groupoid stackification with a torsor-and-equivariant-map quotient
in its algebraic setting. Our parameter site is the sheaf-context site
described above; no transfer of that proposition's geometric hypotheses is
claimed.
