## Source results and boundaries

Heunen, Kammar, Staton and Yang, *A Convenient Category for Higher-Order Probability Theory* (LICS 2017), §§IV–V and VII.A, construct function spaces and a probability monad for quasi-Borel spaces. Proposition 22 gives commutativity and a comparison with measurable-space kernels, with an isomorphism qualification for standard Borel spaces. Theorem 26 makes the space of kernels a quotient of the space of random functions, not an isomorphism. This is a candidate change to the ambient mathematical setting when higher-order random values are required. It does not identify a distribution on functions with all its pointwise marginals. [Author PDF](https://www.cs.ox.ac.uk/people/samuel.staton/papers/lics2017-qbs.pdf).

Vákár, Kammar and Staton, *A Domain Theory for Statistical Probabilistic Programming* (POPL 2019), uses quasi-Borel predomains. Theorem 4.6 supplies a commutative statistical powerdomain; Theorems 6.4 and 6.9 establish compositionality and adequacy for SFPC. The direction needed here is denotational equality implying contextual equivalence, not full abstraction. Corollary 6.11 permits independent sequencing reorderings with the displayed typing conditions. This adds recursive types, recursion and soft constraints to the comparison; the pure probability monad alone is not being credited with all these capabilities. [Author PDF](https://www.cs.ox.ac.uk/people/samuel.staton/papers/popl2019.pdf).

Paquet and Winskel, *Continuous Probability Distributions in Concurrent Games* (MFPS 2018), Theorem 4.10 constructs a bicategory with **race-free standard Borel games** as objects. Theorem 5.2 gives ground-result adequacy for the affine probabilistic language specified in §3.1. Section 6 explicitly distinguishes a primitive fair coin from thresholding a normal sample: their Boolean distributions agree, while their strategies differ. The model is deliberately intensional. Section 3.1 excludes practical features such as general inference; the conclusion leaves non-affine generalization as further work. This source cannot justify unrestricted duplication or a distribution-preserving quotient compatible with every context. [Author PDF](https://www.cl.cam.ac.uk/~gw104/mfps18.pdf).

These are bounded full-text inspections of the named constructions and results, not independent audits of every proof. No inference algorithm or Lean implementation was tested.

## Proposed matched comparisons

**P1: retain a random function's joint behavior.** Fix a function sampled once, passed to a client, then applied at two inputs. As a finite diagnostic, sample a fair Boolean `b`: one random function returns `b` at both inputs; another returns `b` and its complement. Pointwise distributions agree, while the first pair is always equal and the second always unequal. This is our elementary discriminating example, not a theorem attributed to the papers. Require both routes to state whether their representation retains that joint law. Add a continuous random affine function with real coefficients and a measurable two-evaluation observer; do not replace its distribution with a finite approximation without an error contract. Compare quasi-Borel value/function spaces plus probability computations with the existing kernel route extended by explicit random environments. Failure of ordinary kernel exponentials does not by itself disprove a closure-converted or first-order environment representation; compare what each author must expose and prove. Conversely, a bare space of pointwise kernels fails the joint-law contract.

**P2: distinguish mathematical output from interaction.** On a shared affine fragment, compare a fair coin with a thresholded normal draw. Freeze two contracts separately: output distribution and retained event/strategy structure. Ask for a compositional observation map on the admitted fragment, not an assertion that ground adequacy makes every internal distinction irrelevant. Attempt one observer that counts or identifies internal sampling events. A quotient that equates these programs must reject that observer or strengthen its contract. The external strategy route can fail if it cannot support the selected pure extensional equality under the demanded contexts. No general compositional quotient theorem is claimed here.

**P3: add recursion only after the observation contract is fixed.** Compare the same recursive random specification under an ordered probability model and an explicit kernel approximation route. Separate convergence/sound approximation from exact equality, and state normalization failures at zero/infinite weight. This is a later extension of P1, not a reason to make the first comparison implement all SFPC.

## Priority and unresolved alternatives

P1 is a concrete challenge to the broad D5 proposal: it tests a combination of capabilities that neither probability-only nor higher-order-only success settles. It deserves an explicit place in the cross-workstream decision, using #199's already-specified experiment instead of presenting its existence as new evidence. A new issue is justified only for its unowned experiment scope, not to repeat closed #199. P2 belongs alongside the already-scoped behavioral-versus-independent-meaning comparison #285; it should not duplicate that whole issue or presume its chosen fragment includes probability. P3 is deferred unless recursion changes the decision about P1.

This supplement does not compare probabilistic coherence spaces, valuation-domain models, computable probability or general concurrency models in sufficient depth. Those are live alternatives. If a required observer is nonmeasurable, if unrestricted recursion is essential at the first boundary, or if retaining interaction is mandatory, reassess the choice of P1's two routes before adoption. The reason for this recommendation is a capability-combination counterexample and inspected models, not a claim that quasi-Borel spaces are universally best or easy to formalize.
