## Modifications are a consequential distinction

Mathlib's `Pseudofunctor.StrongTrans.Modification` (`Bicategory/Modification/Pseudo.lean:51`) stores a 2-morphism at each base object and its naturality equation. Those 2-morphisms need not be invertible. The category and `isoMk` are already supplied by Mathlib. `StackDescentTransformation.modification_naturality` is the ordinary-component form of this exact equation, obtained with `congrArg` and the Cat whiskering formulas; its inverse-comparison counterpart is derived by cancellation.

There is actual, small overlap with `CoGrothendieckMap.mapModification`: its naturality proof also takes `congrArg (fun k => k.toNatTrans.app ...)` of the modification naturality field. A neutral component lemma could serve two mathematically distinct consumers:

1. The map between images of arbitrary descent data, used by `StackLocalGeneration.localModificationData`, extension of modifications, and mapping-category recovery.
2. The vertical total natural transformation used by coherent-family classification recovery over context changes.

This is more than two adapter presentations of one example. However, the shared portion is only extraction of an already supplied Mathlib law; the descent overlap proof and the total identity/composition-constraint proof remain different. The existing descent lemma is a few lines, and the total consumer already keeps the same law as a local equation. A new module would add an import and a maintained wrapper name without deriving a new coherence obligation. My recommendation is **retain now**, with no mandatory follow-up. If the coordinator selects it, keep the old descent theorem as an alias and require both actual downstream proofs to consume the helper; do not claim that a shared statement alone removes the two larger proofs.

The larger alternative, a common heterogeneous transformation-and-modification framework, would need identity/composition, vertical modification composition, whiskering, unit/counit recovery and bridges to Mathlib strong transformations. Those obligations are real and not discharged by moving them into fields. No inspected client requires that new framework: the homogeneous coherent-family route already preserves modifications, and the heterogeneous sheaf route already derives its required recovery. This is evidence to retain both rather than a general impossibility claim.

## Mapping-category recovery must remain intact

`StackLocalGeneration.extendModification` constructs a modification over arbitrary target objects from locally presenting source objects. Its proof includes independence of source image, restriction/refinement compatibility, common-cover descent, naturality and source recovery. These are consequences of full faithfulness, local generation and target prestack structure, not ordinary inverse-square coherence. `fullyFaithfulRestriction` at line 495 therefore recovers full hom sets between transformations.

`StackificationUniversal.extension` and `extensionRecovery` (lines 3308 and 3343) add the transformation-level extension with its composition/unit equations. `universalEquivalence` at line 3374 then gives `(B ⟶ T) ≌ (F ⟶ T)` for every target stack, under the actual full-faithfulness, locally-generating and base-pullback hypotheses. The forward functor is the original restriction. Do not replace this by fibrewise equivalences, essential surjectivity on objects, or an equivalence of iso-classes.

`Examples/ActionStackification.localUniversal`, `chartUniversal`, and `chartModificationEquiv` (lines 104–120) retain the #678/#679 contracts. The chart and globally-trivial presentations are useful preservation controls for those public APIs, but they are **not counted here as two distinct mathematical families justifying extraction**. The cross-family evidence comes from descent and indexed-family/totalization uses.

The coherent-family `totalEquivalence` uses `Equivalence.mk`, and separately proves verticality of the packaged adjusted unit. The sheaf total equivalence proves its triangle directly and keeps its original unit. A common wrapper must preserve this distinction; do not identify the original and adjusted units merely because both project vertically.

## Alternatives and scope controls

**Mathlib first.** Continue using its `StrongTrans`, modification category, descent data/pull functors, `Functor.FullyFaithful`, ordinary equivalence-mate constructors, native CoGrothendieck map and map composition isomorphisms. The existing code already does so. The recently added `BasedDiagram` handles diagram restriction via Mathlib based functors and original recovery; duplicating that machinery is outside #723.

**Small transport lemmas.** Existing inverse-square transport is the strongest cross-family case. Simplifying fixed-source variants through that implementation can reduce shared proof while preserving normalization explicitly. The two arbitrary-cartesian preservation proofs in `CoGrothendieckMap` and `CoGrothendieckTransport` are another bounded candidate, subject to the use lens's check of concrete coherent-family versus local-sheaf applications. A generic helper should take a base-preserving functor plus preservation of selected cartesian lifts and derive preservation of arbitrary cartesian arrows, using Mathlib uniqueness and vertical isomorphisms. It must not assume the final arbitrary-preservation statement. Merely testing two encodings of the same total map would not meet the distinct-consumer requirement.

**Broader framework.** Do not adopt now. Stack descent needs arbitrary cover/refinement data and modifications; sheaf totalization needs heterogeneous native fibre universes and arbitrary context arrows. A broad interface that asks callers to prove all existing laws adds no demonstrated derivation, and a leaner interface that omits either side loses used information.

Relevant existing controls:

- `Examples/TorsorStack.empty_descent_not_faithful` proves that dropping covering information loses distinct loops. `loops_distinct` proves retained automorphisms survive legitimate descent and classification.
- `Examples/ActionStackification.not_effective` and `not_isStack` establish a prestack input with noneffective object descent; `chart_not_effective` retains the labelled-chart case. Thus totalization must not be called stackification or assumed to add gluing.
- `Examples/CoherentFamilyFibration.collapseTotal_not_iso` and the decoded collapse component retain a noninvertible map. `badData_no_recovery` rejects removing coherent restriction conditions.
- `Examples/CoherentFamilyFibration.Ordered` checks both noncommuting substitution orders (the displayed fibre sizes differ), their composition, cartesian lifting and recovery naturality. The local-sheaf Boolean examples retain their own nonidentity substitutions and composite-collapse controls.
- Stackification's `ParameterComposition.effectivePullComp`, `pullValueIso_comp` and `extensionComparison_comp` retain actual composite parameter transport. These are existing formal statements inspected in source; no fresh claim that all coherence is redundant is made.

These controls are relevant to any selected refactor. A new failure-of-coherence example is not mandatory for an unchanged ordinary-equivalence extraction when existing controls exercise the exact risks. If a new interface weakens laws, construct a model meeting the weakened inputs but violating the intended consequence before accepting it.
