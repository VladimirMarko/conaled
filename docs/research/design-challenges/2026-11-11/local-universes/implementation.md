# Local universes: implementation and use assessment

Retain both weak-unit routes and the Type-family implementation. The next concrete constructor worth testing is dependent Π in the existing Type-family presentation. It shares exactly the parameter type already used for Σ, while its direct comparator already exists in `Dependent.lean`. This can justify a small shared parameter helper. It cannot by itself justify a common groupoid/Type framework or project-wide adoption of local universes. A subsequent dependent groupoid constructor would test the currently unmeasured benefit of generic selection where direct strict stability is harder.

This assessment inspected revision `e80c51d968925be3809ba3acc3259de7041b504b` in the pinned source checkout. References below are relative to that checkout. No source was changed and no Lean build or experiment was run. Proposed constructions are paper designs, not checked results.

## Existing builders and consumers

`Applications/LocalUniverses/WeakUnit.lean:41–55` makes lift targets and arrows actual data. Builders supply `NormalizedLifts.lift`, its image equation and identity normalization. They do not supply a composition law. The direct route constructs arrows by `conjugate` (line 33), proves projection using `directIso_image`, `branch_map`, and `leg_natural` (lines 97–137), and proves substitution by examining the chosen endpoint lifts (lines 152–182). A caller supplies the fixed presentation `p`, its lifts, motive `c`, branch `d`, and an equality of branch functors `h`. It receives an entire functor, with beta, projection and substitution laws.

The parameter route adds the parameter category and groupoid, the commuting-arrow equation, evaluation, generic branch, naming, inverse equations, and naming naturality (lines 189–318). That setup is mathematically useful: `representation` (line 377) is an equivalence on whole functors, and `parameter_substitution` (line 341) follows from `name_substitution` plus composition. Its stable operation does not choose a new filler on each substitution. However, `generic` (line 320) is built by applying `direct` to the generic motive. The present implementation therefore establishes reusable naming and selection machinery, but has not demonstrated that this machinery reduces the work of constructing an additional semantic operation. Exact agreement in `instantiated_agreement` is for this same supplied lift datum.

The caller in `Examples/WeakGroupoidUnit.lean` constructs `nonsplit`, then uses `direct` for `repaired`, consumes universal beta/projection/composite-substitution laws, and consumes exact generic agreement in `native_generic_agreement`. Its arrow observations are substantive: `retains_branch_arrow` retains the C₂ loop, `distinct_fillers` separates equal object maps, and `swaps` together with `fixes_whole_branch` distinguishes base substitution from automorphism invariance. These are strong reasons to retain this implementation and its observations through any extraction. The source search found no second application importing the weak-unit theory beyond this example and the public integration path.

`SquareKit/LocalUniverses.lean` has a different, smaller interface. `Family` stores a parameter type, fiber family and name; `reindex` changes only the name. `sigma` (line 71) constructs its parameter object explicitly. `sigma_reindex` is reflexive equality, and `pair_reindex` reuses the native `DependentSquares.pair_subst`. `displayCurry` and `displayCurry_natural` (lines 109–119) already construct a dependent section representation and its naturality. A framework must reuse this existing proof rather than request its conclusion as an extra field.

There are actual consumers beyond the local examples. `SquareKit/Internal/DesignatedMap/Interpretations.lean`, namespace `Represented`, uses `Family.comprehensionEquiv (Family.present ...)` for `represent`; `pair_unique` derives uniqueness from that equivalence. `Examples/LocalUniverses.lean` retains a dependent Fin-valued second component through two nonidentity substitutions. Its `PresentationFailure.presentation_ne` proves that equal decoded families can have unequal parameter presentations. `Examples/LocalUniversesBoundary.lean` retains separate generated-source transport and higher-witness obligations; its module comment explicitly does not identify them with a local-universes interpretation. These consumers would gain nothing from being required to construct a weak-unit `Parameter` groupoid.

## A concrete additional constructor

A bounded Π experiment can reuse the following parameter data of `Family.sigma`, for `A : Family Γ` and `B : Family A.Comprehension`:

```text
P := Σ a : A.Parameters, A.fiber a → B.Parameters
n γ := (A.name γ, λ a. B.name (γ,a))
Πfiber (a,k) := (x : A.fiber a) → B.fiber (k x)
```

The proposed `Family.pi A B` is `(P, Πfiber, n)`. Its decoder is the existing `DependentSquares.piTy A.decode B.decode`. The helper shared with Σ would consist of `P`, `n`, evaluation of a dependent name at a fiber element, and the name's substitution equation. The sum and product fibers remain different operations.

The direct comparator is already explicit in `Dependent.lean:98–106`: `piTy`, `lam`, and `app`. Beta, eta, formation substitution, abstraction substitution and application substitution appear at lines 136–165. In this Type model they are proved by reduction. Consequently a claim that local-universe Π removes difficult direct coherence work would be false for this comparator. Its potential benefit is avoiding duplicate parameter construction between Σ and Π and retaining presentation-level strict stability when clients already use `Family`.

At the paper level, define abstraction and application through native `lam` and `app`, as `Family.pair` already delegates to `pairTerm`. Derive their substitution equations from the existing native laws and the proposed decoder equation. Do not add substitution fields to a new Π model record: the current operations already determine the proofs. Do not require identity normalization or a cleavage for these Type operations.

The mathematical obligations here are explicit. Type supplies the dependent sum forming `P`, function spaces `A.fiber a → B.Parameters`, and dependent products for `Πfiber`. Existing `Comprehension` and `comprehensionEquiv` supply the dependent context and its representing pairing. With all data in `Type u`, these finite dependent sums and functions remain in that same universe level. No classifier for every small family is introduced. `displayCurry` supplies the section bijection for a fixed display and arbitrary base map. This bounded construction does not need a new ambient LF theorem, general pullback choices, or a full comprehension-category record.

A groupoid Π follow-up has different obligations. The fixed shape `I` in weak-unit `curry_substitution` does not vary with the first family, whereas the proposed Π's second name ranges over a varying fiber. Reusing that exact currying theorem cannot discharge the dependent representation problem. Such an experiment must construct the relevant dependent section parameter, its arrow action and naturality, then construct the semantic Π family and lambda/application operations with their laws. If a full-slice dependent-exponential theorem is used instead, its exact hypotheses and size closure must be supplied and checked. This report does not establish that theorem from the current groupoid code. The existing `MissingTerminal.no_terminal` control also prevents treating every current source `Model` as already supplying all ambient finite-product assumptions.

## Small corrections and migration costs

No established correctness defect emerged in the selected constructions. The immediate correction is to distinguish three demonstrated facts: direct weak-unit stability, parameter-route stability derived from naming, and Type-family Σ stability. None establishes a general coherence provider for existing source interpretations or a measured reduction in author effort.

A small extraction should preserve the public `Family` operations and use a transparent helper for the shared Σ/Π parameter construction. This would preserve the existing reduction behavior used by `rfl` examples. Replacing those definitions with abstract equivalences or opaque operations could create elaboration and transport obligations for the Fin example and the `Represented` consumer; that is a migration risk to measure, not an observed failure.

For groupoids, a generalization of ordinary currying naturality from fixed `I` to an arbitrary fixed category shape is plausible shared infrastructure. It should remain a candidate until a second caller uses it. Extracting all of `Parameter`, or forcing Type families through it, would carry strict pullback equations, natural transformations and groupoid instances into clients that currently use ordinary functions. There is no second concrete caller here demonstrating that cost is repaid.

A future generic-filler API could take a constructed filler and its beta/projection equations, deriving its substitution law from naming. It must keep the current `generic p L` constructor as a specialization; merely accepting the desired stable final eliminator would move the burden without solving it. No uniqueness or full automorphism-equivariance law should be added: the C₂ controls show the intended selected observations cannot support those demands.

Comparison/descent obligations remain separate. Fixed-presentation naming does not identify two presentations, and `presentation_ne` makes that distinction observable even in Type. Any broader adoption claiming transport between presentations must provide its comparison on types, terms and dependent witnesses. Existing comparison and descent callers should retain their own hypotheses and observations until such a construction exists; the weak-unit result does not supply it automatically.

## Discriminating evidence

The smallest experiment is to add the proposed Type-family Π in isolation, using exactly the existing Σ inputs and native Π comparator. Prove decoded agreement, presentation-level formation substitution, lambda/application beta and eta, and two successive substitutions with a genuinely dependent tail. Reuse `DependentSquares.Example.AS`, `BS` and `tighten` to ensure the tail depends on the selected Fin element. Recheck the existing dependent Σ and designated-map comprehension callers unchanged. Record which shared helper actually has two callers and whether the abstraction introduces explicit casts or additional parameters. Successful completion supports that helper's extraction; it still does not rank general foundations.

For broader adoption, run a second trial with a varying groupoid domain and nontrivial arrows. Require a dependent parameter representation with both inverse equations and naturality on all substitution functors, a constructed operation, repeated-substitution equality on arrow maps, and a direct comparator where available. Include a non-faithful substitution and preserve the C₂ weak-unit regressions. Count constructor-specific obligations separately from reusable representation proofs. The result can favor direct construction, local universes, or a different semantic foundation; matching the incumbent interface is not an admission condition.
