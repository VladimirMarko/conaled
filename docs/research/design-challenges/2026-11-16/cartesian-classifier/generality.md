## Decisive cofibration correction

The candidate's first paragraph says Cof=Ω imposes a stronger filling requirement than the finite face/diagonal formulas. That conclusion does not follow from choosing the larger-looking presentation. On the stated cube category, the following direct argument shows every sieve on a represented cube has such a finite formula, classically.

Write an arrow f:m→n as an n-tuple of endpoints and variables. Enumerate its k distinct variables. Factor f as m→k→n, where p:m→k selects those distinct variables and i:k→n inserts endpoints and repeats selected variables in the original pattern. The map p has a section: assign its selected coordinates the k variables, and assign unused coordinates 0. Consequently f belongs to a sieve S on n if and only if i belongs to S. The principal sieve generated by i is exactly the conjunction setting each constant output coordinate to its endpoint and equating all output coordinates that repeat the same variable. There are finitely many patterns i up to renaming their k variables: partitions of the n output positions, with some blocks fixed to 0 or 1. Thus S is the finite union of the principal sieves for its included patterns. Classical logic decides which of the finitely many patterns to include.

This is a new paper argument, not a checked Lean theorem. It uses precisely the endpoint/variable syntax inspected in `SquareKit/Internal/Cubical/CartesianDimensions.lean:9–49`, and the category definition already frozen in `docs/research/second-cubical-witness-preservation.md`, “Fixed source and target.” It does not rely on concurrent #601 implementation results. Applied to every represented chart, it also covers #600's stated general-context convention of compatible local formula subobjects. It would not establish equality with a globally finitely generated subobject convention on arbitrary contexts; #600 does not use that convention.

Consequence: the manuscript should either include this argument and identify the two extensional cofibration domains, or remove the asserted strict strengthening and use the finite-formula domain directly. If composition is indexed by raw formula syntax rather than extensional subobjects, an extensionality/representation independence condition is additionally needed; this is a presentation issue, not evidence for a smaller mathematical domain.

Even without this equality argument, the finite-formula alternative already has its needed theorem in the primary source. [ABCFHL §3.2, Proposition 1, p. 42](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~rwh/papers/uniform/uniform.pdf) proves closure of the face lattice under interval quantification by generic-variable elimination. Its elimination sends x=x to true, x=r with r independent of x to false, and retains equations independent of x; it distributes through conjunction and disjunction. Thus the candidate's final paragraph unnecessarily leaves this as an absent proof. The same source explicitly permits Ω classically and its Theorem 2 applies to either presentation. These facts support retaining the classifier construction while correcting its claimed restriction.

A discriminating check is to formalize the split factorization and equality of its generated sieve with the equation conjunction. At dimension one it must recover exactly empty, either endpoint, both endpoints, and the total sieve. At dimension two it must include the diagonal and its intersections with sides; omitting diagonals would invalidate the general argument. These tests distinguish the actual Cartesian category from categories with extra algebraic interval operations.

## Higher consumer and groupoid alternatives

The B(Z/2) consumer survives the all-sieve choice. The accepted report's nerve filler, `cartesian-filling-recomputation.md:127–168`, works for arbitrary groupoids, and its construction tests only closed-vertex membership in the tube sieve. Its tube law uses functoriality of the supplied tube, so this is not an unsupported inference from vertex objects alone. The candidate uses this nerve as a decoded type over terminal, rather than confusing the finite-set code nerve with its discrete decoder.

The separating observation is sound at the stated paper level. A square in the nerve with constant vertical arrows forces equal horizontal arrows. Hence loops 0 and 1 at the sole object are not relatively homotopic. For the displayed h_z formula, addition telescopes under composition of arrows, the extra coordinate difference vanishes at each endpoint slice, and evaluating at the object yields z. A hypothetical relative comparison of E_0 and E_1 therefore projects to the forbidden square. Subject to the externally supplied natural ua/coe homotopy, the universe two-path separation follows too. This does not assert uniqueness of arbitrary higher fillers.

A classifier restricted to groupoid nerves could accommodate this particular consumer, but would reintroduce a presentation restriction for arbitrary input families. Thus it is a useful specialization or a smaller implementation milestone, not a substitute for #602's declared-domain classifier. Conversely, the Z/2 example alone does not demonstrate non-groupoidal decoded homotopy types: it establishes the issue's required separation from finite-set local systems. The generic represented classification argument, rather than this example, carries the broader claim. No additional higher-homotopy example is required by the issue as written.
