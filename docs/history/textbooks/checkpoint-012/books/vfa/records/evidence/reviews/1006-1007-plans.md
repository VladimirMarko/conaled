## Assessed concrete risks

- **Source equations and fuel:** Selection preserves both remainder-order
  branches, ties, zero fuel, empty input, and the separate length-fuel and
  genuinely well-founded definitions. The proposed length formula and fuel
  saturation are consistent with those equations. The plan retains exact-fuel
  premises, both source length-equality orientations, redundant `n` wrappers,
  and the optional contents theorem's actual output `selection_sort l`.
  It distinguishes the nine-element well-founded example from the
  eleven-element pi example. No unconditional permutation of under-fuel
  execution is claimed.
- **Termination and split identity:** Merge retains alternating placement and
  the singleton-left rule. Strict decrease is restricted to lengths at least
  two; zero/singleton behavior is explicitly covered. The proposed ceiling/
  floor lengths and their sum match the source recurrence. The existing
  contiguous split is not substituted. Both well-founded sorts retain
  equations and meaningful induction/equation consumers.
- **Proof routes and pedagogical failures:** The failed Selection definition
  and aborted Merge proof are explained without admissions. Selection's
  optional contents route remains a genuine alternate argument, with no
  fabricated tactic ban. Merge correctly permits the book's alternative bag
  route while requiring the resulting occurrence permutation. The exact
  two-step induction schema and its conditional use are retained;
  `List.twoStepInduction` really does have the additional one-cons induction
  hypothesis that the proposed adapter can ignore. `sorted_merge1` retains
  its weaker premises about the merged output; it is not replaced by a
  theorem requiring individually sorted inputs.
- **Stability, fixed points, and equality:** The tagged Selection trace
  `[A(2),B(2),C(1)] → [C,B,A]` is correct, and does not contradict its
  separately proposed sorted-input fixed point or idempotence. Alternating
  merge sort's equal-key three-record two-cycle is also correct and refutes
  generic record stability, fixed points, and idempotence. Both plans label
  these as key generalizations, not distinguishable Nat duplicates.
  Nat output uniqueness and equality after mapping to ordered keys are
  separated from equality of records. Merge stability remains relative to
  concatenated sorted blocks.
- **Measured counts and boundaries:** Selection's select count is tail
  length; the two-parameter fuel recurrence and triangular full count fit
  the actual recursion. The five-element fuel-three/full counts are nine
  and ten; the stated quadratic two-sided estimate is valid for `n≥2`.
  Merge's split count, merge comparison upper bound, height bound `n*k`
  under `n≤2^k`, and sort-call count handle their stated base cases.
  The plans require proofs connecting these specifications to instrumented
  execution. Comparison/visit counts are not equated with complete runtime;
  local overhead, comparator representation, allocation, sharing, GC,
  auxiliary array space, and empirical speed claims are qualified.

interfaces and pinned Mathlib `Data/List/{Induction,Sort}.lean`. The inspected
file hashes match those recorded in the plans. In particular:

- `sourceMerge_eq_merge` and `mergeBy_source` need executable comparison,
  not sortedness; their equality is about values, not costs.
- `mergeBy_sorted` uses a linear preorder on keys. `mergeBy_filter` and
  `mergeBy_stable` use a preorder and decidable key equality, with both input
  sortedness premises. `mergeBy_spec` also needs decidable key equality.
- `List.Perm.eq_of_pairwise'` needs antisymmetry of the element relation;
  `StableSort.unique` instead uses key antisymmetry together with stability.
  The plans do not confuse these two routes for tied records.
- Existing `Resources.Measured` composition and
  `Resources.isBigO_of_nat_bound` support the proposed use; neither supplies
  the missing measured recurrences or a native-runtime bound. Their source
  identities also match the plans.
