## Tree-prefix machine: independent meaning and actual finite program

### Representation, callbacks and fixed control

`TreeParserMachine.lean:20–110` defines three scratch/raw stack kinds plus an output indexed by destination, a protected stack family, five owned phases, two distinct results and caller labels. The stack alphabet is Tag on raw/reverse/output, Unit on pending, and the supplied protected alphabet on caller stacks. Temporary registers are exactly `Option Tag × Option Unit`; the caller register is a separate preserved factor. The concrete client proves seven stack identifiers, fifteen labels and sixty-eight register values for two destinations, two protected stack kinds, one extra caller label and a Bool caller register. The actual twenty syntheses confirm the relevant finite and decidable instances, including every dependent alphabet branch.

The finite `block` is an actual `TM2.Stmt` construction. Init pushes one pending token. Scan observes the actual raw head; N consumes the tag, records it on reverse and pushes a pending token. E consumes and records its tag, pops pending, then observes whether a token remains to choose scan or emit. EOF and foreign tags enter undo without consuming the blocked head. Emit moves the actual reverse contents onto the selected output and takes its empty probe before the live success exit. Undo restores reverse material onto raw; dropPending clears actual remaining pending tokens and takes its empty probe before the live failure exit. Every jump resets both temporary fields while retaining the caller register.

The installed `program` dispatches owned phase labels to those fixed blocks and external labels to the supplied caller. Machine callbacks use finite register observations and fixed constructors. They do not execute list lengths, natural-number fuel decodings, `treeCode`, the source parser, `scanSteps`, `runSteps`, a validity recognizer, or a semantic oracle. Those functions appear on the proof/specification side. In particular, `resultCfg` computes a specification endpoint from the source result, and `runInTime` supplies proof-side timing data; neither is called by a transition block.

The finite program is fixed before arbitrary raw words and protected contents are quantified. `machine` supplies a genuine `FinTM2` with the same `program`, and `machine_step_eq` identifies its actual step. `initList_eq` connects its actual input representation to the ordinary initial configuration. No eventual halt, final Boolean-output cleanup, complete verifier or NP theorem is claimed for this open component.

### Literal configurations and generalized transfers

The actual `cfg`, `cfg_ext` and `cfg_eta` describe all dependent stack fields, register fields and labels. The one-step theorems are about `TM2.step (program caller)`, not an abstract evaluator. They quantify arbitrary raw words, reverse words, pending tokens, old destination outputs, protected stacks and initial registers. This matters for corrupted states: a scan with empty pending can enter emit after E, which the ordinary source entry invariant intentionally excludes.
