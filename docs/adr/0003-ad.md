# ADR 0003: differentiate through certified representations and curve relations

Status: accepted

## Context

Elliott's [The simple essence of automatic differentiation](https://conal.net/papers/essence-of-ad/)
calculates a category of differentiable functions and then changes the
representation of derivatives to obtain forward and reverse modes. A square
that merely forgets the tangent component is insufficient: it also commutes
for an arbitrary, wrong tangent implementation.

The [author's errata](https://conal.net/papers/essence-of-ad/#errata-and-clarifications)
correct two relevant points. `onDot` applied to an arbitrary function on
covectors returns a function, not automatically a linear map. In the opposite
and continuation constructions, cartesian structure uses cocartesian
structure in the underlying category, and conversely.

[Higher-order, higher-order automatic differentiation](https://conal.net/papers/higher-order-ad/)
is explicitly an early, incomplete November 2019 draft. The
[complete literate source](https://github.com/conal/paper-2020-higher-order-ad/blob/master/hoad.lhs)
distinguishes higher derivatives from higher-order functions. It also explains
why a represented function may need derivative information, and why a first-
derivative representation alone does not complete its proposed closure
construction. Its general lazy tower composition and closure algorithms are
unfinished. We must not promote those proposals to proved theorems by omitting
their analytic assumptions or by calling every function space a normed space.

## Decision

Use Mathlib's actual real Fréchet derivative as the specification. The AD
modules import the pinned Mathlib version; the existing compiler and square
kernel remain independent of Mathlib.

### First-order differentiable category

`Applications/AD/Core.lean` defines `DMap E F` with a primal map, a continuous
linear derivative at each point, and `HasFDerivAt` at every point. Identity,
composition, constant maps, projections, pairing, addition, and nonlinear
multiplication discharge their proof obligations locally. Category and
product laws are equalities of certified maps. Derivative uniqueness supplies
the equality of derivative components when primal functions agree.

`tangentSquare` is the ordinary object-level square. `tangent_correct` proves
the extra condition that its tangent output is the real derivative applied to
the input direction. `linearCompositionSquare` and `linearPairSquare` expose
the derivative-representation operations as ordinary commuting squares.

### Representation changes and generalized compilation

`Applications/AD/Modes.lean` supports arbitrary continuous linear seed maps in
forward mode. Reverse mode represents a linear map by precomposition of
covectors. The continuation is itself a continuous linear map, obtained from
continuous bilinear composition. Its composition theorem reverses order; its
pairing theorem adds the two reverse contributions. These are the corrected
cartesian/cocartesian roles.

`onDot` deliberately has the corrected ordinary-function signature. Hilbert-
space adjoints have their own square from vectors to covectors. Its proof uses
the defining inner-product equation, and `gradient_correct` gives the
directional-derivative property of the returned gradient. Completeness and
inner-product-space assumptions appear in those theorem signatures.

`Applications/AD/Compiler.lean` supplies the existing `FirstOrderCompiler`
with a `DMap` target. Its object interpretation is the original real value
type: scalars, the zero-dimensional unit type, and nested products. The
independent source evaluator appears in `compile_hasFDerivAt` and in the
forward/reverse correctness theorems.

`Applications/AD/Generalized.lean` goes further: the same source compiler
constructs derivative representations directly. `LinearModel` contains local
relational squares for the linear operations. Both `direct` and
`continuations` discharge every field. A `Program` stores a computation
returning a primal and derivative code; the actual mathematical derivative is
an existential witness only in `Prop`. Thus the proof does not add a semantic
derivative value to the runtime representation. The `modes_agree` theorem
compares independently constructed forward and reverse representations on
every covector and direction, for every source term.

### Higher-order source functions

`Applications/AD/HigherOrder.lean` reuses the original simply typed compiler,
including function types, lambda, application, products, let, and sums. Its
base representation is a dual number. Correctness is a logical relation
between dual values and real-valued curves at an observation point.

At a scalar the relation contains value equality and `HasDerivAt`. At a
function type it requires preservation of the relation for every related
argument curve. This includes simultaneous variation of a captured function
and its argument. The fundamental lemma proves preservation for every source
term. Arithmetic primitive obligations are discharged. The compiler theorem
then yields an actual derivative at every scalar observation.

Sum curves use a fixed constructor along the whole curve. This is an explicit
restriction of the relation. The theorem is not an AD rule for branch-switching
comparisons or discontinuous conditionals. The example
`twice (lambda y => x*y+x) x` differentiates the captured `x` as well as the
argument and yields `3*x*x+2*x+1`.

This interpretation avoids an unsupported choice of topology on all function
spaces. It does not claim a cartesian-closed category of Banach spaces,
reverse-mode function-type cotangents, or a realization of every proposal in
the incomplete draft.

### Higher derivatives

`Applications/AD/Jets.lean` completes a polynomial fragment of the tower
idea. A finite polynomial represents all orders of its derivatives.
`derivativeSquare` connects symbolic to analytic differentiation.
`jet_correct` proves the correspondence for every natural-number order;
`jet_successor` supplies an actual `HasDerivAt` certificate between adjacent
components. `jet_leibniz` is the all-order binomial convolution rule. A separate
expression language includes variable, constants, addition, multiplication,
and composition; `compile_jet_correct` proves every derivative order of its
independent evaluator.

This representation is exact for polynomials and eventually zero above their
degree. It makes no convergence assertion about Taylor series and is not the
draft's general coinductive algorithm for arbitrary smooth functions.

## Consequences

The correctness contracts compose at three distinct levels: source
compilation, analytic differentiation, and linear derivative representation.
Their interfaces let another backend refine derivative code without changing
the analytic theorem or the source syntax.

The use of real numbers and general continuous linear maps makes the
analytic modules mathematical implementations in Lean; they are not a claim
of efficient executable IEEE floating-point AD. Rounding error, overflow,
cost, numerical stability, allocation/sharing, and code generation require
additional representations and proofs. In particular, the `let` structure in
`Program.comp` permits shared primal computation, but no runtime cost theorem
is asserted.

All newly stated mathematical claims are Lean declarations, with no `sorry`,
`admit`, or added axioms. Standard Lean/Mathlib foundations, including classical
choice used by real analysis, remain part of the trust base. Reusing a proved
analytic chain rule is intentional; treating it as an unchecked backend axiom
would not be equivalent.

## Validation obligations

The project build checks all six implementation modules and the axiom audit in
`Applications/AD/Verification.lean`. The key declarations are:

- `DMap.derivative_eq_fderiv`, `tangent_correct`, `tangent_square_comp`;
- `continuation_comp`, `continuation_pair`, `adjoint_square_comp`,
  `gradient_correct`, and `mixedPolynomial_true_derivative`;
- `FirstOrder.compile_hasFDerivAt`, `FirstOrder.compile_forward`,
  `FirstOrder.compile_reverse`, and `FirstOrder.sharedPolynomial_derivative`;
- `Generalized.compile_derivative`, `Generalized.compile_reverse_correct`,
  and `Generalized.modes_agree`;
- `HigherOrder.eval_related`, `HigherOrder.compileDual_hasDerivAt`, `HigherOrder.compileCurveSquare`, and
  `HigherOrder.higherOrder_hasDerivAt`;
- `Jets.jet_correct`, `Jets.jet_leibniz`, `Jets.towerMulSquare`,
  `Jets.jet_zero_above_degree`, and
  `Jets.compile_jet_correct`.

Source provenance: the full
[2018 literate manuscript](https://github.com/conal/essence-of-ad/blob/master/essence-of-ad.lhs),
the author's errata, and the full 2019 draft were read for the relevant
constructions. The errata take precedence over superseded source definitions.
